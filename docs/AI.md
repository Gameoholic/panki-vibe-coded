# Panki's AI — the plan

> **Scope of this file.** The AI epic: what the owner wants from it, what's settled, the order it's built in, and what's still open. Short on purpose: it's the plan that survives a cleared chat. Ticked as steps land; a step's own design is talked through when it's reached, never ahead.
> **Write here:** decisions, the order, open questions. **Don't write here:** how a built step works inside (→ `ARCHITECTURE.md`), what it does for the owner once built (→ `PRODUCT.md`).

Started 2026-10-30, as a conversation: the owner is product, Claude is the engineer. Nothing of an earlier attempt is used; this starts from scratch.

## Why

The owner's words, 2026-10-30, none of it concrete ("I'm just throwing stuff, not all of it will stick; it's your job to make it stick"):

- **Transcripts aren't 100% accurate.** A model reading the transcript could fix misheard words.
- **Meanings.** A dictionary's meaning is sometimes off, or no sense fits the sentence.
- **Context, grammar, usage.** "Like a human conversation when I talk to my Japanese friends": roughly what it means and when it's used, where the dictionary can't say. Guides, never just translates: "that's the entire point of Panki."
- **The sentence.** Trimmed to only the context needed to read it without the clip. Maybe made-up extra examples, with a scenario line ("Picture you asked Takashi for help with the exam but want to be low-key about it:").
- **Customizable, everything.** Prompts never hardcoded; templates edited in a UI with the care of the SRS editor. The admin picks the model. The owner: "I'm a freak" for customizability.
- **Surgical.** One clear AI, integrated, toggleable, never "chunk AI into everything." Admin sees usage and cost as with Forvo and ElevenLabs.

## How every AI action is built

The owner, 2026-10-31: "I want the AI flow and whatever to be consistent and not a different method or UI or flow for every AI feature." Every action, today's and every one to come, is built this one way; one that can't be is talked through first.

1. **One registry of actions, templates under them.** An action is one entry of `AI_ACTIONS` (contracts `ai.ts`); what it sends is a template (`AiTemplate`, since step 5): each account's own in its database, or one of Panki's read-only starters (`aiTemplates.ts` → `AI_STARTERS`), a prompt and a Follow-up prompt each a `PromptField`, edited in Settings → Panki AI with the SRS editor's care. The server fills a template by its id, never text the page sends; the action's button runs the account's default (`defaultTemplateOf`).
2. **One Prompt context.** Every template draws from the one registry of pieces (contracts `pieces.ts`), filled by `fillTemplate`, cut by `piecesAsSent`, its tags the pieces' own names.
3. **A fixed shape for every answer.** Each call answers in a Zod shape (`ExplainShape`, `ExplainFollowUpShape`, `PlainShape`, `touchUpShape`, `touchUpFollowShape`), every key `describe()`d for a template's Panki's answer format (`answerShapesOf`, one tree per shape, headed by which call answers in it); the descriptions are never sent. One word for one thing across shapes: why the model answered as it did is always `reason` (a meaning row's, a proposed meaning's, a field proposal's; Touch up's said `why` until the owner, 2026-10-31: "you drifted from EXPLAIN for no reason"), and text Panki puts somewhere is what it names (`value`, `meaning`).
4. **One reader.** `apps/api/src/ai/index.ts` → `answer`: the schema sent each provider's way, bounds and descriptions stripped (`schemaFor`, `UNSENT`); a cut, a refusal, broken JSON or the wrong shape is one failure, metered all the same.
5. **A follow-up answers in its action's own follow-up shape: its reply, and only what it changes** (step 5; a one-key `{ "answer" }` until then): Explain judging the meanings `ExplainFollowUpShape` (the rows again only when the question changes them), Touch up `touchUpFollowShape` (`changes`, a field each, and the Meaning), a template that doesn't judge `PlainShape` throughout. What it sends is the template's Follow-up prompt filled (`{Follow-up question}`, `{Follow-up field}`, Touch up's `{Card}` as the card stands now), handed back as `asked`; the history goes whole, as it was sent (`Said`).
6. **Panki's rules on top, as refines.** What no schema can say (one row per meaning, a proposed meaning only when none fits) is a `superRefine` whose reason is "Expected … Got …" (contracts `unread.ts`, the one home for that wording; `explainRuleBroken`, `explainFollowUpRuleBroken`, `plainRuleBroken`, `touchUpProblem`, `touchUpFollowProblem`).
7. **The meanings in one format.** Sent as the JSON list `{"meaning","index"}` and nothing else, never what's ticked on the card, so the model can't anchor on the pick (`meaningsPiece`; `selected` until the owner, 2026-10-31), judged back a row each, fits / close / wrong with a reason, and a proposed meaning when none fits (`MeaningAccuracy`, `ProposedMeaning`); what's shown is Panki's reading of those rows, never a verdict word the model chose. Explain and Touch up alike (Touch up since 2026-10-31, step 4b). Wherever a judged meaning is listed it's drawn by one piece, `JudgedRow` (the pop-up's rows, the drawer's Meaning list): the row its own size, the mark on its words, its reason under a chevron, closed to begin with.
8. **What the AI writes is marked and accepted.** Every value carries its source `ai` (`ValueSource`), and wherever it's shown (`byAi`) it's in the AI's ink (`--ai-ink`) with the spark once, out of its words: in the review card's corner, on its deck row, before its name where the layout shows the name, inside a field's box (the owner, 2026-11-04); nothing lands on a card until the owner accepts it; a proposal of what's already there is drawn as "Nothing to change.", never as a proposal (`fieldVerdictOf`; set aside with a note of its own until 2026-10-31).
9. **One head over every answer.** `AiStatus` (the spark, "Panki AI", the status button, the cost) and its Details: the fixed rows always open, then Prompt context and the Log as alike folds, closed at first; the Log is the whole exchange and, on a failure, the answer as it came and the error note (`LogFailure`).
10. **One failure wording.** What anyone reads is plain, "Panki AI's answer couldn't be used. Try again." (`ai/index.ts` → `UNREAD`); the technical reason lives in the Log alone (`AiUnread`).
11. **Cost before and after.** "Up to $… (est.)" beside the button before it runs (`ceilingMicros`), what was metered after; a failed answer is metered all the same and Details say so; every call is one usage row (`shared.meter`).
12. **The spark is one component.** `AiSpark` (`components/AiWaiting.tsx`) on every button that asks the AI, every head and every mark: `burst`, a one-time sparkle on the press; `working`, the eased half turn on the button and the head while a call is on its way (`--spark-turn`). Never the `ai` icon drawn bare, never its motion written in other CSS.
13. **No skeleton for an AI wait.** The turning spark and Thinking are the wait; only words being reworked are dimmed under the sweep (`AiWaiting`).
14. **Run again at will.** The button is pressable whenever it isn't thinking; a rerun is a new call, the earlier ones kept as chips in Details.
15. **Text only.** What a model writes is text everywhere it goes (`CONVENTIONS.md` → Security).
