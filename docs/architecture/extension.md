# The extension (steps 16, 17 and 19)

> **Scope of this file.** The browser extension as a whole; its larger parts each have a file of their own beside this one.

> **Never run by Claude.** Everything here was written from the browsers' documented interfaces and passes `pnpm typecheck` and `pnpm build`. What is most likely to be wrong is marked "unproven" below. The owner loads it and says what's off.
>
> **What the owner has seen work (Chrome, 2026-10-11):** signing in, a deck set up, the line on a YouTube video, the panel, and a card added to the deck. The recording produced a file. The clip's way to Panki failed on that first try and was fixed (the lore below, "cut at its last comma"); whether a clip now arrives and plays is the next thing to hear. The owner's first note on it, with a screenshot of auto-captions on two rows: the line under the player showed only the bottom row. Changed the same day to show all of it and to take whole sentences; not yet seen by the owner.

`apps/extension` is one codebase for Chrome and Firefox (Manifest V3). `scripts/build.mjs` builds the same files into `dist/chrome` and `dist/firefox` and writes each its own manifest (they differ only in Firefox's id). The worker is named both ways in one manifest, `service_worker` and `scripts`: Chrome from 121 runs the first and passes over the second, Firefox from 121 the other way round, so the zip Panki hands out (Chrome's build) loads in Firefox too. Until 2026-11-05 each folder named it its own browser's way alone, and Firefox refused the zip: "background.service_worker is currently disabled. Add background.scripts." (the owner's screenshot; they load the zip, never a folder built here). It's loaded by hand: Chrome → Extensions → Developer mode → Load unpacked → `dist/chrome`; Firefox → `about:debugging` → Load Temporary Add-on → `manifest.json` of either folder (gone when Firefox closes: staying needs Mozilla's signature, which is the owner's call). **Unproven in Firefox:** all of it: the manifest is the first thing it reads, and nothing past it has run there (`optional_host_permissions`, `storage.session`, `scripting.registerContentScripts` and `mozCaptureStream` are each written for it from their documentation).

The drawer shows only the decks until then. The per-way `last` is gone, and with it `Way`. (2) **The mapping is read the other way round.** The options page's "Field mapping" lists the deck's fields and each gets what fills it; the stored shape is still piece → field id (`FieldMap`), so nothing kept was lost: `roles.ts` → `roleOf` and `withRole` turn it about, and a field takes one piece and a piece one field. It saves as it's chosen. Nothing is guessed any more (`suggestMap` and the ready-made card type `READY_TYPE` are gone, with `panki.addType` and `typeMaps`); every field starts on None. (3) **The pieces are what the extension can fill by itself, and nothing else:** `word` (Selected word), `sentence`, the four recorded off the video, each a registry entry with a `cut` (`of`: the sentence's clip or the word's own stretch; `soundOnly`): `clip` (Sentence video, the id maps from before already hold), `clipAudio` (Sentence audio), `wordVideo`, `wordAudio`; and `source` (Link to the video, into a YouTube link field only: the line of words it used to be is gone, with `Capture.sourceLine`). The reading and the meaning are no longer pieces; `FieldMap` drops a key it no longer knows instead of failing, so a map from before keeps the rest. (4) **Every field of the card that holds words and that nothing fills is typed in the drawer** (`Panel`: `typed`, by field id), in the card type's own order, required or optional as the type says; the word's field is typed too, starting from the pick. (5) **Word audio is a second cut of the video** (`youtube.tsx` → `Cut`, `wordStretch`, `Taking.wordTrim`): the picked word's own stretch, from the beats its characters fall in, with the sentence's lead and tail; its ends are pulled, typed and heard exactly as the clip's are (the same `Trim`; the same `ClipOffer`; `moveEnd`, `hearCut`, `toggleCut` take which cut). **Its bar is the clip, end to end** (`ClipOffer.within`, `Trim`'s `within`: the bar shows exactly that stretch and has no edges to pull), and its ends are kept inside the clip's (`youtube.tsx` → `inside`, and `moveEnd`'s limit for the word). **What's recorded is the mapping's to say:** the drawer has a bar per stretch and records once for every mapped piece of it (`Panel` → `recorded`, `cuts`), with its picture or as sound alone as the piece's `cut.soundOnly` says; there is no "Sound only" switch any more. A stretch going into two fields is two passes over the video. Another word starts it over (`wordTurn`). Captions shown a whole line at a time give only the line's time, so there its ends are put by hand. (6) **Audio and Video fields are one kind, Media** (below).

**What it may do, and why so little.** `permissions`: `storage`, `unlimitedStorage` (the dictionaries, step 19), `contextMenus`, `scripting`, `activeTab`. It runs by itself on one site only, until the owner allows looking words up on others (step 19: the browser is asked for those sites on the options page, and only then is `page.js` registered there). Anywhere else nothing of it is on the page until the owner acts (the menu, the button), and then only in that tab (`activeTab`). The one address it ever calls is Panki's, which isn't known at build time: `optional_host_permissions` only lists what may be asked for, and the browser asks the owner for Panki's own host when it's typed on the options page (`address.ts` → `hostPattern`: the host without its port, since a pattern can't name a port in Firefox and covers every port without one). No file of the extension's is ever fetched by a page (no `web_accessible_resources`), so a page can't detect it by asking for one.

**Who holds what.** The session's secret lives in `chrome.storage.local` and is read only by the worker and the options page (`storage.ts`); a page's script never touches storage and never calls the API. **Profiles (2026-10-17, the owner: switching between a real Panki and one for trying things, on the extension alone).** The sign-in in use stays `address` / `token` / `userName`, so the worker reads nothing new; the other sign-ins wait in `profiles`, each a bearer session of its own on its Panki with a name of the owner's own (`name`; the one in use keeps its in `profileName`, set at sign-in or renamed on the row, `renameProfile`), and the Account tab switches by swapping one in (`useProfile`: the one in use goes among the kept, a kept one of the same account at the same address is replaced). Switching tells Panki nothing; Sign out on a row ends that session and drops it. A `401` from the one in use forgets it alone (`forgetSignIn`), the kept ones staying to switch to. Deck mappings and the default deck are keyed by deck id, so two Pankis' never collide; everything else kept is this browser's, shared across profiles on purpose. It asks the worker (`messages.ts`: `state`, `add`, `upload`, `options`, `deck`, `subtitles`, `prefs`, for the dictionary `lookState`, `lookup`, `lattice`, and for transcribing `transcript`, `piece`, `fix`, `forget`), every message is a Zod shape parsed where it lands, and the worker answers only its own extension's senders. The worker is woken per event and keeps nothing in memory between them.

**On a page that isn't Panki's.** `ui/mount.ts` makes an element with a **closed shadow root**: the page's scripts can't read into it, the page's styles don't reach in, the app's don't leak out. The styles are the app's own files, imported as text (`ui/styles.ts`: `theme.css`, `app.css`, then `ui/extension.css`), with `:root` rewritten to `:host` and the `@font-face` blocks dropped (their files sit under the app's own address; on another site they'd be asked of that site), so the panel is built of the web's real `Button`, `TextField`, `Form`, `Toggle` and looks like the app by construction. Its font falls back to the device's. Keys and presses are stopped at the host, because from outside a shadow root a key typed into a field doesn't look typed into a field, and a player would take every letter as a shortcut. **Unproven:** that YouTube's own key handling listens late enough for that stop to beat it; and that a page with a strict style policy lets the `<style>` in (Chrome does for an extension's script; Firefox is the question).

**Reading the line (`capture/captions.ts`).** Nothing is fetched and nothing of the player's is called: a `MutationObserver` on the player's caption area reads the words already drawn, and stamps each line with the video's own time as it appears and as it goes. The last few lines are kept (`KEEP`), in the video's order; a line seen again where it was (a rewind, a clip played back, the replay that records it) is the same line, not a new one, and keeps the end it had; a line cut off by a jump ends where the video was, not where it landed. **Seen again means two things** (`read`): the whole of its text about where it was, or only the beginning of it inside its own time, because the player writes a row of its own captions out a word at a time every time round. Without the second, each time a clip was played back its lines were taken for new ones, and since only the nearest few are kept they would in the end have pushed out the very lines being added. `writing()` says which line is on screen being written right now, for the first time or again. A line is **heard out** when the captions moved on from it, and only **cut short** when the video jumped away part-way: both have an end, but a cut one's is just where the video was, so while it's being written again `unfinished()` names it and a sentence that runs to its end waits for it, exactly as for a line written for the first time. **A caption shown whole is one line; the site's own listening captions roll up a row at a time and are written a word at a time, and there each row is a line**: the bottom row is the one being written, each stretch it gains is stamped with its time (`marks`), and the rows above it are matched back to the lines they were (`shown()` is what's on screen, top to bottom, or what was last there). **Under the player Panki has two rows of its own, each the whole width** (`youtube.tsx` → `rowsUnder`, `Row`). The player's rows are short, made for the middle of a video, so each of Panki's holds several of them end to end: how many (`perRow`) is the room measured off the rows themselves (a `ResizeObserver`, in full-width characters) divided by the longest row the player has written in this video (`longestRow`, which only grows). **A line's row is fixed by its place in its stretch of talk** (`Line.run`, `Line.seq`, given once in `captions.ts` → `add`; a silence longer than `NEAR` starts a new run at 0), so words never move sideways once drawn: a row fills from the left, and when it's full it moves up whole (keyed by run and row) and the next begins, the way the player's own captions roll, only wider. Exactly two rows' height is always kept and a lone row sits on the lower one; a row never wraps (what it holds is counted to fit, and the overflow is cut sooner than a third row is made), except in a window too narrow for even one of the player's rows. Nothing later than the line being written is drawn, so going back over a video doesn't show what's still to come. It took four tries on 2026-10-11, each from the owner's screenshot: only the player's bottom row ("way shorter", cut off too quickly); both rows joined as one text (it "stops here" halfway across, and the whole line jumped left as rows scrolled off: "jarring"); the player's rows mirrored one for one (a third row "for a split second", because the player holds three while one slides out, and "so much space it can utilize to the right"); then this. **The lesson: read the player's rows for what was said and when, never for how to lay it out.** A pick is kept as its place in what's under the player read as one text (`transcriptOf`) and carried between readings by line (`reread`), because a sentence runs across rows and the rows move on while the panel is open. **What's taken is a sentence, not a row** (`youtube.tsx` → `sentenceOf`): the lines that were on screen are read as one text and the sentence around the picked word is found by its full stops (`sentenceAround`, the same reader a page's selection uses), across rows; with no word picked it's the last sentence, the one being spoken. Its time comes from the marks (`timeOf` where its first words appeared, `timeAfter` where the words after it did), so a clip covers the sentence and not the rows it happened to be drawn on. A sentence that runs to the end of what's shown is let finish: the video stops when the bottom row is over (and is started, if it was stopped by hand part-way through it, so its clip has an end). The class names are the site's and are gathered at the top of `captions.ts` and `youtube.tsx`: **they are the first place to look when the line stops showing.** The line is split into words by the dictionary when there is one (step 19, below), and by the browser's `Intl.Segmenter` otherwise (`capture/words.ts`), which splits finer than a dictionary would, so a pick can be widened to the piece beside it. Each word is drawn in a box of its own (an inset shadow, not a border, so a re-split moves nothing after it by more than a hair; `ROOM_USED` counts the boxes' room).

**The clip (`capture/record.ts`).** The player's own `<video>` is captured (`captureStream()`, afresh for each recording and let go after; Firefox's `mozCaptureStream()`, which takes the sound away from the speakers, so it's played back out through an `AudioContext`, kept while its picture is live) and written by `MediaRecorder` as webm (VP9 or VP8 with Opus; Opus alone for sound). Chrome's stream was kept for the page's life until 2026-10-18, when clips made on prod came out as 110-byte files, a WebM's header with nothing in it, while the sound cuts of the same cards were whole: most likely the kept stream carrying the sound on after its picture had stopped (the site changes video without a new page; a clip made after the tab was reloaded was whole). A recording with no Cluster in it (`holdsMedia`) is refused as "Nothing came out of the player", never kept. **The player is hands-off while it's recorded** (2026-10-19, the owner: "if I touch it at all… it stops recording, goes back to the card creation panel and says the error"): from the moment it plays the stretch, a `pause` (but the one the video makes at its own end), a `seeking`, a `ratechange` or an `emptied` on it stops the recording (`TOUCHES`, `RecordTouched`); the drawer then makes no card whatever the field (a Required one or not), says why, and is ready for Add again. A browser can't hand back what already played, so the stretch is played once more: the video is paused, set to normal speed, moved to where the clip begins, recorded to where it ends, and put back. Which stretch that is, is the drawer's business (below); the recorder is handed two times. Thirty seconds at most. **Everything that makes it again is kept with it** (2026-10-28, the owner: "save all the data we can… so in the future I can re-record batch on client side"): the recorder gives back how it came out with the file (`RecordFacts`: where the video really stood when it stopped, the picture's size and rate as the player played it, the bit rates it was given), the page makes that the file's `MediaRecording` (`youtube.tsx` → `recordingOf`: the video's address, which cut, the stretch asked for), and the worker sends it right after the file with the extension's client and running build (`PUT /api/media/:id/recording`, `media_recordings`; its failing is said in the worker's console and never stops the card). A sound keeps what's said in it as a clip does (`media_cues`), nothing showing it. What else was known as the card was made goes on its source (`CaptureFacts`, `card_sources.capture`: the page's `takenFacts`, the drawer's dictionary word and field roles, the worker's build, `background.ts` → `stamped`); a part that doesn't read as the contracts have it is left out rather than stop the card. The recording is carried to the worker as text (a message can't carry bytes) and kept with `PUT /api/media/:id` (the `upload` ask); the card is made after it, naming the file in its field, so a Required clip field is filled as the card is made. (Until 2026-10-17 the card came first and the clip followed it by `PATCH`, so a Required clip field couldn't work at all and the panel said so; the owner met that message with a clip right there.) A recording that fails stops the card when its field must be filled, and is left out with a note otherwise. **Unproven:** that `captureStream()` on YouTube's player gives picture and sound (the fallback, Chrome's tab capture, is not built), and the whole Firefox path.
