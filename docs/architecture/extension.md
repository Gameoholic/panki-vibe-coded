# The extension

> **Scope of this file.** The browser extension as a whole; its larger parts each have a file of their own beside this one: `transcribing.md`, `dictionary.md`, `watching.md`, `ai.md`, `kept.md`, `updates.md`, `forvo.md`.

`apps/extension` is one codebase for Chrome and Firefox (Manifest V3). `scripts/build.mjs` writes `dist/chrome` and `dist/firefox`, differing only in Firefox's id. **The worker is named both ways in one manifest** (`service_worker` and `scripts`): Chrome runs the first, Firefox the second, so the zip Panki hands out (Chrome's build) loads in Firefox too; a manifest naming it Chrome's way alone is refused by Firefox. Loading it by hand is `RUNNING.md`'s. A browser loads the worker and a page's script each as one plain file, so each is built alone in Vite's library mode as an IIFE; `setup.ts` is the first import of every entry. Unproven in Firefox past the manifest: `optional_host_permissions`, `storage.session`, `scripting.registerContentScripts`, `mozCaptureStream`.

| Part | Built as | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `src/background.ts` | `background.js` | The worker: holds the sign-in, the only thing that calls Panki (`panki.ts`), answers the pages' scripts by message, hears the menu and the toolbar button |
| `src/content/youtube.tsx` | `youtube.js` | On `https://www.youtube.com/*` by itself (top frame only): the Panki panel, the drawer, the clip, the transcript, the seek bar's marks, the player bar, YouTube's own pages |
| `src/content/page.tsx` | `page.js` | Put in a tab when asked (the menu, the button) to capture a selection; registered by the worker on the sites the owner allows for the Word pop-up |
| `src/options/main.tsx` | `options.html` | The extension's own page |

## What it may do, and who holds what

- **It asks for little on purpose** (`build.mjs`'s manifest and its comment). It runs by itself on one site only; anywhere else nothing of it is on a page until the owner acts, and then only in that tab (`activeTab`), or on sites the owner granted for the pop-up. The one address it calls is Panki's, unknown at build time: `optional_host_permissions` lists what may be asked for, and the browser asks for Panki's host when it's typed (`address.ts` → `hostPattern`: the host without its port, since a pattern can't name a port in Firefox and covers every port without one). No `web_accessible_resources`, so a page can't detect the extension by fetching one of its files.
- **The worker is the one way to Panki and to the dictionaries.** The session's secret is in `chrome.storage.local`, read only by the worker and the options page. A page's script never touches storage and never calls the API: it asks the worker (`messages.ts`), every message a Zod shape parsed where it lands; the worker answers only its own extension's senders. The worker is woken per event and keeps nothing in memory it can't lose.
- **What's about YouTube is set here, even the account's own settings.** The options page reads the account's settings once a sign-in (`options/useAccountSettings.ts`, in `Options`) for the Header bar panel's Streak shown, inside the Streak's row (`PillPanel.tsx` → `HeaderBarPanel`, `PartRows`' `moreOf`, `pillStreak`), and the Streak reminder panel with its Starts at (`StreakReminder.tsx`, `reminderOnYouTube`, `reminderHour`), each saved a moment after it's changed (`useSavedSoon`, `PATCH /api/settings`); a YouTube page reads them again as it gets the focus. Panki's Settings has none of them.
- **The YouTube tab's order**: Video tracking, Transcribing, Spoken word underline, Seek bar marks, **Header bar** (on every page of the site, so never under the home add-ons' switch; `homePill` keeps its stored name), **Streak reminder** (shown by both the header bar and the home, held by neither), YouTube home add-ons, YouTube utilities, Player bar. The header bar's (?) (`explainerScenes.tsx`) plays on a video's page, the reminder's on the home, the camera on the header bar's Streak and then on the line.
- **The header bar's Streak** (`ui/PankiPill.tsx` → `streakValue`): its flame `--yh-muted` until this period counts (`StreakState.now`, `is-pending`), `--mark-orange` once it does, and tinted `--risk` with the hours left while the reminder is on for it (`is-risk`).
- **Profiles**: the sign-in in use is `address` / `token` / `userName`, so the worker reads nothing else; other sign-ins wait in `profiles`, each a bearer session of its own, swapped in on the Account tab (`useProfile`). Switching tells Panki nothing. A `401` forgets the one in use alone (`forgetSignIn`). Deck mappings and the default deck are keyed by deck id, so two Pankis' never collide; everything else is this browser's, shared across profiles on purpose.

## Places on a page

- **Every place of Panki's is a closed shadow root** (`ui/mount.ts` → `mount`, `makePlace`): the page's scripts can't read in, the page's styles don't reach in, the app's don't leak out. One React screen draws into every place through portals, so what's picked in the line and what's in the drawer are one state.
- **The styles are the app's own files** (`ui/styles.ts`: `theme.css`, `app.css`, then `ui/extension.css`), with `:root` rewritten to `:host` (everything `app.css` declares on `:root` too, or an animation reading `--sweep` has no duration inside and never runs) and the `@font-face` blocks dropped (their files sit under the app's address; another site would be asked for them). So the panel is built of the web's real components and looks like the app by construction.
- **Keys and presses are stopped at the host**: from outside a shadow root a key typed into a field doesn't look typed into a field, and the player would take every letter as a shortcut. Unproven: that YouTube's own key handling listens late enough for that stop to beat it.
- **Closed roots hide events**: a window listener sees a press inside as a press on the host (`useDismiss` listens on the root too); React's `onSelect` never fires inside a shadow root (it asks the document for the focus and gets the host), so the drawer's sentence reads selection itself; `assignedSlot` is null inside a closed root, so a walk up to a scroller stops at the root (`useHoist` → `scrollerOf`). A page script can read into its own closed roots, which is how it tells typing in the panel's fields (`typingIn`).
- **Only Panki's nodes are moved, never the site's**, and only when they aren't where they belong (`youtube.tsx` → `place`, every second): moving a node takes the caret out of a line being typed.
- The class names and selectors of the site are constants at the top of `captions.ts`, `youtube.tsx`, `feed.tsx` and `modules.ts` (`BAR_CONTROLS`): **the first place to look when something of Panki's stops showing.**

## The Panki panel

- **Views** (`modules.ts` → `PANEL_VIEWS`): Under the player (two full-width rows) or Beside the video (the default; only a pick is ever written). The site's full screen puts it beside whatever is picked.
- **Beside the video** (`columnBeside`, `ui/Column.tsx`): the panel goes into Panki's column, first in `ytd-watch-flexy #secondary`, as tall as the player, after the drawer while that's open. The column's shadow root holds one scroller with a slot, so the drawer and the panel scroll inside it as one and the drawer opening never makes the column taller than the player. **The column counts only while it's really beside the player, read off where both are drawn**, never the site's attributes for theater mode or a narrow window (there the site puts the column under the player, and the panel stands under it too). Nothing of the site's is taken away: the recommendations stay under it. The drawer's dropdowns are `anchored`, opening in the window, so neither the column nor a `Fold` cuts them off. **Its width is the column's left edge, dragged** (`Column`'s grip, `Modules.panelWidth`, kept by the worker; null is the site's own): held inside its bounds so the video keeps `PLAYER_MIN_WIDTH` (`panelWidthOf`), on the page by one stylesheet of Panki's (`layWidth`): beside the video the site's column is made that wide through the site's own variable for its width, only while Panki's column really stands in it (`html.panki-beside`), and in full screen through `--panki-stage-w`; the player is told the window resized so it sizes its picture to the room left. Unproven: that the site's layout follows its sidebar variable when it's overridden.
- **The list** (`BesideList`): every line of the transcript, or of the captions seen so far; the line the video is at lit, the list scrolled (never the page) to put it a third of the way down only once it's the second-to-last line in view, so a line standing a little off its place moves nothing; a scroll by hand holds it for `FOLLOW_HELD_MS`. Everything that reads "what's under the player" reads the whole list (`underLines`), and a capture takes the lines around a pick (`aroundPick`). Each line is a memoized `ListLine` with handlers read through a ref, so only the line being said redraws; `useWords` asks `AT_ONCE` lines at a time and keeps `KEEP`.
- **The head and its one menu** (`ui/PanelMenu.tsx`): the menu's rows change in place (Subtitles, Panel, Panki), rows rather than sub-menus, because beside the video the panel stands at the screen's edge, where a menu flying out sideways opens off screen.
- **Under the player** (`rowsUnder`, `Row`): two rows of Panki's, each the whole width, each holding several of the player's short rows end to end (`perRow`, from the room measured with a `ResizeObserver` over the longest row the player wrote). A line's row is fixed by its place in its stretch of talk (`Line.run`, `Line.seq`), so words never move sideways once drawn; a full row moves up whole; exactly two rows' height is kept; a row never wraps. Nothing later than the line being written is drawn. **Read the player's rows for what was said and when, never for how to lay it out.**

## One full screen (the Stage)

With Panki on and the panel on, the site's own full screen, entered any of its ways, is Panki's: the video fills the screen but for the panel's column (`youtube.tsx` → `fullHere`, `stageNow`, `STAGE_CSS`). With either off it's the site's, untouched.

- **Read from `document.fullscreenElement` alone** (on `fullscreenchange`, again a frame and 300 ms later as the site lays out, and every look), **never from a flag of Panki's saying it asked.** It's Panki's when what fills the screen holds the whole watch page laid out as the site's full screen (`ytd-watch-flexy[fullscreen]`), never the player alone.
- Then `html` carries `panki-stage`, and CSS put on the page once holds the site's `#full-bleed-container` beside `--panki-stage-w`, hides the header and stops the page scrolling; the window is told it was resized so the player sizes its picture. **The player's node is never moved.** Panki's column goes inside the full-screen element (only what fills the screen is drawn). Left, the page is scrolled back where it stood (`scrollWindowed`, kept all along, since the site enters full screen before Panki hears of it).
- **Folding the panel** (`togglePanel`, its key `Modules.panelKey`, Hide panel in the head): folded, the stage's layer comes down and the full screen is the site's own look; folded is kept per browser, and a card begun while folded brings the panel up for as long as the drawer is open, never kept. The button that brings it back (`ui/PanelButton.tsx`) is fixed inside the full-screen element at its top-right corner over everything of the site's (inside the player, the site's layers covered it), left of the site's own corner buttons (`.ytp-chrome-top-buttons`), fading with `ytp-autohide`.
- **The page's keys** (`PAGE_KEYS`: the panel's, the subtitles' and the player bar's, `watching.md`) are heard in the page's one capturing `keydown` listener after the short skip: by `e.code` too, so another keyboard layout still reaches them; a letter with Shift never (Shift+P and Shift+N are the site's); a lone modifier on its `keyup` with nothing else pressed; never while typing on the page or in Panki's fields; never Esc, the browser's for leaving full screen. The panel's only while the panel stands in a full screen; P is its default because YouTube's own shortcuts leave it free. The subtitles' with a video and the panel on; C is its default, taking the site's own captions key on purpose. The player bar's with a video while the bar is Panki's (Panki on or off, as the bar is), set on the Player bar panel; X is its default, a key the site leaves free.
- **Fragile**: the site's full-screen element holding the watch page; the `fullscreen` attribute on `ytd-watch-flexy`, `#full-bleed-container`, `#masthead-container`; anything of the site's fixed above `z-index` 2101.

## The capture drawer

`ui/Panel.tsx`. Top to bottom: Add (and the Panki AI block under it), the clip, the deck, the typed pieces, the sentence, the card as a review will show it. × and Escape close at once, whatever was typed. On any other page the drawer is fixed down the window's right side (the left when the selection would be under it).

- **The moment the card is in** (`onAdded`), the video page lets go of the capture as closing would (`youtube.tsx` → `letGo`): the video on from the latest of where it stands, where it stood and the clip's end (never back into the clip), then playing or paused as After adding a card says; the clip's marks and stop gone while the drawer still says Added.
- **Nothing is chosen for anyone**: no deck until one is chosen on purpose (`defaultDeckId`), no field mapped until the owner maps it, a deck's mapping off until switched on. **Every field that holds words and that nothing fills is typed in the drawer**, in the card type's order, required as the type says.
- **The card is the web's own** (`components/ReviewCard.tsx`), so the preview is the review's card by construction. `standIns` draw what isn't a value yet: the clip is `ui/ClipPlayer.tsx`, the player's bar under a canvas the page's video is drawn onto, **drawn only while the video is free or playing its own cut** (`held`), or it mirrors another cut. Its subtitles are a `StandIn` `ReviewCard` calls, so the rule stays the card's. `ReviewCard` takes `srcOf` rather than importing the web's API client, so no network code is pulled into a page's script.
- **The sentence lights where the word is** (`whereIs`): a box can't light part of its own text, so `TextField`'s `mark` draws the text again behind a see-through box, always, so the box is never made anew. A word not in the sentence is said so and doesn't stop the card (it's often kept in its dictionary form).
- **How it comes and goes** (`components/Fold.tsx`): a grid row taken between `0fr` and `1fr`, the only way both browsers animate to "as tall as its content", ending on `animationend` with a timer behind it. The drawer's room above and below lives inside the fold (`--panki-gap-above`, `--panki-gap-below`), or it would jump. A drawer opened again starts from `lastKnown` and asks behind it; with nothing to go on it waits unseen up to `SLOW_MS` rather than showing a line and growing. Keys go to the first field a frame after it's ready.

## A field that holds a link

The `source` piece goes into a YouTube link field and nowhere else: the video's address at the second the clip begins (`youtubeUrl`), following the clip's start floored to the second, so it opens up to a second early, never late. Empty off a video.

## What fills which field

`roles.ts`: one registry of what the extension can fill by itself (the selected word, the sentence, the recorded cuts, the link, and the dictionary's kinds, `DICTIONARY_ROLE`), each saying what fields can hold it. The owner's card types are theirs, so nothing is known by a field's name: a `FieldMap` (piece → field id) per deck, cleaned against the live type every read (`cleanMap`), shown from the field's side (`roleOf`, `withRole`), dropping a key it no longer knows rather than failing. `mappingOn` is the one reading of the switch: off unless switched on, but a deck mapped before the switch existed and never switched counts as on.

## Reading the line

`capture/captions.ts`: nothing is fetched, nothing of the player's called. A `MutationObserver` on the caption area reads the words drawn and stamps each line with the video's time as it appears and goes.

- **Seen again is the same line**: its whole text about where it was, or only its beginning inside its own time, since the player writes a row out a word at a time every time round. Without the second, a clip played back makes new lines that push out the very lines being added.
- A line **heard out** has its end; one **cut short** by a jump ends where the video was, and while it's written again `unfinished()` names it so a sentence running to its end waits.
- **A caption shown whole is one line; the site's rolling captions are written a word at a time, a row each line**, each stretch stamped (`marks`).
- **What's taken is a sentence, not a row** (`sentenceOf`, `sentenceAround`, the same reader a page's selection uses), its time from the marks. A sentence that runs to the end of what's shown is let finish.
- Split into words by the dictionary when there is one, by `Intl.Segmenter` otherwise (`capture/words.ts`). Each word is a box drawn with an inset shadow, not a border, so a re-split moves nothing by more than a hair.

## The clip

- **A stretch of time; its words follow it** (`youtube.tsx` → `clipOf`). Until an end is touched, the clip is the sentence around the pick with a lead and tail (`asSaidOf`); once an end moves both are written in seconds and the words are derived from the time (`beatsOf`, `firstHeld`, `lastHeld`), so an end a tenth of a second either side of a word doesn't change the sentence. An end can go anywhere in the video (`limitOf`); words beyond the lines taken are read from every kept line it reaches.
- **Said or shown** (`timedAsSaid`): a transcript's words are timed as said, each with its own end, and a word is held when more of it is inside the clip than out (`holds`), the lead and tail stopping at the neighbouring words; captions are timed as shown, a touch late, and keep the nearest-with-lead rule. A pulled end goes exactly where it's put; Alt snaps to beats' edges. This rule is on trial: one commit ("Clip timing: …"), to revert if worse.
- **The sentence can be locked**: typing in it locks it; unlocking is the undo, the clip's words again. Another line taken unlocks it.
- **Word audio is a second cut** (`Cut`, `wordStretch`): the word's own stretch, its ends pulled exactly as the clip's, kept inside the clip (`inside`). What's recorded is the mapping's to say: a pass per mapped piece, with picture or sound alone as `cut.soundOnly` says.
- **Hearing it is the page's own video** (`capture/replay.ts`), watched every 30 ms because `timeupdate` comes four times a second and overshoots by a word; whatever plays stops at the clip's end and nowhere before. Closing puts the video back where it stood, unless it's playing by then. Played by hand, the video stops at the clip's end once (`stopAt`, `stopSpent`).
- **The bar keeps still** (`ui/Trim.tsx`): `ROOM` seconds either side from the moment it opens, changing only when its own edges are pulled, another sentence is taken, or an end lands outside it.
- **A clip with no words** (`stretchOf`): `STRETCH` seconds up to where the video stands, the same `Clip` shape, so nothing downstream knows the difference.
- **Recording** (`capture/record.ts`): `captureStream()` **afresh for each recording** and let go after (a stream kept across the site's video changes gave empty files); Firefox's `mozCaptureStream()` takes the sound off the speakers, so it's played back out through an `AudioContext`. `MediaRecorder` as webm; a file with no Cluster (`holdsMedia`) is refused, never kept. A browser can't hand back what already played, so the stretch is played again: paused, normal speed, moved, recorded, put back. **The player is hands-off while recorded**: a `pause` (but its own end's), `seeking`, `ratechange` or `emptied` stops it (`TOUCHES`) and no card is made. Thirty seconds at most. The drawer's Cancel aborts an `AbortSignal` handed down to `recordStretch` (`RecordCancelled`, the video put back as after any recording); a file already on its way lands unheeded (`Panel.tsx` → `cancelled`), and once the card itself is asked for nothing looks for it.
- **Everything that makes it again is kept with it**: `RecordFacts` → `MediaRecording` (`PUT /api/media/:id/recording`, a failure never stopping the card) and `CaptureFacts` on the card's source; a part that doesn't read as the contracts have it is left out rather than stop the card. The file is uploaded as text (a message carries no bytes) **before** the card is made, so a Required clip field is filled as the card is made.

## On the player's seek bar

`ui/SeekMark.tsx`, a place of Panki's inside `.ytp-progress-bar` edge to edge: the clip with its two ends, how far the transcript reaches and what a run works on, the cards taken from this video (each with its words on a pin, laid in lanes so none covers another), the stop, and the marks of `dictionary.md` → "Words that are in your decks". How each looks is this browser's (`seekLook.ts`, CSS variables on `.seek`).

- **The one spot Panki stands inside the player**: nothing of the player's is called or changed, an element is added to its bar. The place lets every press through (`pointer-events: none`) but those on the clip's two ends, stopped at the place so the bar under them doesn't seek.
- **The reach is read from where the pointer is against where the bar is drawn** (a `pointermove` on the document, once a frame), not from the bar's own events, so nothing is laid over the picture and a click there still pauses. A bar the player has hidden (`ytp-autohide`) is never on, so no unseen pin can be pressed.
- **The player's preview follows the pin, not the pointer** (`peekCard`): while the pointer is on a pin its moves are kept from the bar and the bar is handed a made-up pointer move at the pin's moment. The one place Panki stirs the player's own script; Panki ignores it (`isTrusted`). Unproven: that the player takes a made-up move.
- The cards are asked (`GET /api/videos/:videoId/cards`) when the video changes, a card is added, and the page gets the focus back.

**Unproven:** a press on a clip end reaching Panki before the site seeks (if the site acts on the way down); `#secondary` being the column and staying put; a canvas drawing the page's video in Firefox.
