# Frontend architecture

> **Scope of this file.** The web app's architecture: its screens, state and how they're wired.

**Every screen has an address** (the owner's ask, 2026-10-10, so the browser's back and forward and a mouse's side buttons walk the app): `routes.ts` is a screen by ids alone (`Route`), written as a path (`routePath`: `/decks`, `/decks/:id`, `/decks/:id/cards/:cardId` (a card open in its row, the address replaced in place as it opens and closes, so back still leaves the deck; the extension opens Panki at one so, `askOpenPanki`), `/decks/:id/settings`, `/decks/:id/review`, `/review`, `/review/:id`, `/add`, `/types`, `/types/new`, `/types/:id`, `/types/:id/layout`, `/srs`, `/srs/new`, `/srs/:id`, `/settings`) and read back (`parsePath`). `go` pushes a history entry; a `popstate` or a reload sets `arriving`, and `App` turns it into its `Screen` (`screenOf`) once the list it names is loaded, landing on the list when the thing is gone. No router library. Whatever serves the built app must answer any of those paths with `index.html` (Vite's dev server does). No store: `App` holds where you are (`Screen`: home, decks, a deck, the review picker, a review and where it came from, add, the card types, one type or a new one, the SRS types, a new one, one being edited, settings), the decks, the card types, the SRS types and the settings, the open deck's cards, whether the new-deck or add-card form is open, which deck the Add screen last used, and what the SRS editor's header says; props down, callbacks up. Every screen that shows a card looks the deck's card type up in the loaded types and hands it down: `CardForm` draws one `TextField` per field (a box of several lines when the field's kind says so), `CardList` and `ReviewView` put the Front fields first (the first one big) and the Back ones after the flip, a Paragraph keeping its line breaks. What each kind is (its word, one line or many) is the `FIELD_KINDS` registry in `fieldKinds.ts`; the ids are the contracts' `FieldKind`, so the API refuses a kind that doesn't exist. Japanese text gets `lang="ja"` by looking at the text (`words.ts`), never by a setting. The fixed places in the bar are the `TABS` list; what each screen's header shows (back and where it goes, the title, the action) is `headFor`. `api.ts` is the only place that calls `fetch`, and it parses every response through the contracts. Adding waits for the API's answer, then puts the saved row into the list (optimistic UI comes when it's worth it). The decks, the types and the settings are fetched again whenever the screen changes (and after a card is added), because their counts are derived on the server; the open deck's cards are fetched when the deck changes and kept from a deck to its review, and a deck is read from the list by id so its counts move with every answer.

**The keys (`keys.ts`), one rule for every one of them:** on a screen (the home, today's cards, the review), a key is taken from another action only when both are the same shape of it (a press, a hold or a double press, as the key's own `holdMs` says, never which action it is: `clash`, the one check `withPart` / `withKey` / `withAdded`, the Hold button's switch-on and every key given to a device that kept its keys from before it existed go through; the owner, 2026-11-02, after the Hold button lost its Space to a clip's Space tap). The only actions that never take each other's key are those never heard together (`apart`: Done listening, Show back of card and Step back, a key that's more than one of them heard by precedence: Done listening's on the Listen phase, Show back of card's on the front, Step back's on the back; `ReviewView`'s and `LayoutView`'s `onKey`); the Hold button and Back to start take nothing while it's off, being heard nowhere. **Shift, Ctrl and Alt by themselves are keys** (2026-11-02, the Hold button on Shift): `keyOf` names one alone "Shift", "Ctrl", "Alt", `KeyField` sets one pressed and let go with nothing else, and it's heard as any key through `useHold`, but only as its own press: a module-level watch on the window (capture) notes each one down and whether anything else went down meanwhile (`ownModifier`); a press or double press of one is decided as it goes up, clean (`begin` waits for its keyup), and a hold of one lets go the moment another key goes down (Shift+A is A's). Auto-repeat while held is ignored as for any key. What the system takes first can't be heard: Shift pressed five times opens Windows' Sticky Keys question, Shift held eight seconds its Filter Keys one, and Alt let go alone can move the focus to the browser's menu (Firefox shows its menu bar) unless the page keeps it. **Keybinds Enabled** (Settings → Keybinds, this device's, `panki.keybinds.enabled`; the owner, 2026-11-02, the whole feature's switch in place of the morning's Keycaps, which only hid the keycaps) has one gate: `useKeyBinds` answers the keys as heard, every action without its key while it's off (`unbound`, which empties whatever the `KeyBinds` hold, so an action added later is covered unnamed; the Hold button stays on, held by a finger or a mouse), and every place that hears a key reads it (the home's door, the review and today's cards, the Layout tab, and through their `Shortcut`s every player's keys and full screen's); the review's Enter, the one key not a bind, asks `useKeybindsEnabled`, which `KeyCap`, `HoldClock` and `MediaKey` ask too. Settings → Keybinds alone reads the keys as set (`useKeyBindsSet`). Until it's set, Off on a phone (`(hover: none)`) and On elsewhere; Keycaps' `panki.keycaps`, and the phone's older `panki.keycapsOnPhone` ("true"), are carried over once.

**Room on the disk (2026-10-11, at the owner's ask: "so I don't have to check manually and worry about it").** Clips are what fill a small VPS. `apps/api/src/space.ts` is the one place that measures: `diskOf` asks the disk under the data folder what's left (`fs.statfsSync`, asked every time, never remembered, since other things fill the disk too), `spaceOf` adds `low` (less than `env.lowSpaceBytes` left: 2 GB unless `PANKI_LOW_SPACE_MB` says otherwise, one home in `env.ts`), `accountSpace` weighs an account's folder and reads its files by kind from its own `media` table. That read opens the account's database **for reading only** (`readOnly: true`), so the command runs no migration and changes nothing under a server that has the same file open. `pnpm space` (`cli/space.ts`) prints it; `createApp` takes `space` as it takes the clock, and `GET /api/space` hands it to whoever is signed in. The web asks with the home's stats and draws `SpaceNotice` above the board while `low`; a failed ask is silent, because a warning that can't be had must never take the home down. Sizes are phrased by the contracts' `sizeWords`, for the terminal and the app alike. **Not built:** refusing an upload when the disk is nearly gone. Today a full disk fails the write and the API answers 500.

**Media: one kind of field for a clip or a sound (2026-10-11, the owner: "remove the type video and audio, replace it with simply media which can be either").** `FieldKind` is `text | long | media | image | youtube`. What files a kind takes has one home, `type.ts` → `mediaKindsOf(kind)` (`media` → video or audio, `image` → image), with `holdsFile(kind)` for the yes or no; the old one-to-one `mediaKindOf` is gone. A file's own kind (`MediaKind`: audio, video, image) is unchanged and is what the `media` table has always kept. **Migration 16** is one statement, `UPDATE card_fields SET kind = 'media' WHERE kind IN ('audio', 'video')`: no card and no file is touched. Since a field no longer says which of the two its file is, **the card does**: `Card.files` (file id → `MediaKind`) is derived on every read, from the `media` row each value names (`db.ts` → `valuesWithMedia`, a left join; `cardsFrom`). The web draws by it (`fieldKinds.ts` → `fileKindOf(kind, value, files)`), so a clip gets its frame at once instead of after its file has loaded; an id with no file behind it is left out rather than shown as words. A file picked by hand is told apart by the contracts' `mediaFor(kinds, type, name)`: what the browser calls it first (a `.webm` it calls sound goes in as sound), then the field's kinds in order; `MediaField` takes `kinds` (what the chooser offers) and `kind` (what's showing). The API accepts a file in a field when its kind is one the field takes. The extension records into such a field as video or as sound, whichever the piece mapped to it is (Sentence video, Sentence audio, Word video, Word audio).

**A card's layout (2026-10-15, the owner's pick of four ways: B, edited on the card itself; https://claude.ai/artifact/DWyM6RaC2xMtSrBMbuxCyf; built, not yet seen).** A card type carries a `layout`: one part per field, in the card's own order (`contracts/layout.ts` → `LayoutPart`): what the front and the back each do with it (`FaceShow`: show, spoiler, hide), its size and colour (words; a picture's or a clip's size too) and whether its words are bold (`bold`, 2026-10-18; it came with the big size alone before, so `layoutOf` reads a part kept without it as bold when it's big), whether its name shows, a sound or a clip as the player or a button (`MediaLook`), whether it plays by itself, its column (`column`, `COLUMNS`: `main`, or `side`, the column at the card's left on a wide review; below), whether it sits beside the one above in its column, (`over`, 2026-10-16) the field whose words it's written over, as their reading, and (2026-10-18) `mark`, the field whose words are lit where they stand in its own (a line or a paragraph naming a line or a paragraph; `ReviewCard` → `marked`, the first appearance, exact), and `subtitles`, whether a clip plays with its own subtitles (a Media part; `ReviewCard` → `subtitlesOf` → `ClipSubtitles`, drawn in `MediaView`'s `over` inside `.media-picture`, in the app's own `Settings.subtitleStyle`, set in Settings → Subtitles on cards). **A clip's subtitles are what was said in it, timed, as it was heard on the video when it was recorded:** the extension works them out from the lines it's reading (`captions.ts` → `cuesOf`: each line said in the stretch, from the clip's start in ms, and when each piece of it is said), sends them with the clip (`askUpload` → `PUT /api/media/:id/cues`, after the bytes), the API keeps them once beside the file (`media_cues`) and every card read carries them (`Card.cues`, by the file's id), and `ClipSubtitles` shows the line being said at the clip's time with the piece being said underlined (`cueAt`). For a few hours that day `subtitles` named a field whose words lay over the whole clip, and the subtitle style was the account's, shared with the extension; the owner had the speech followed instead, and the two styles kept apart. A clip recorded before has no cues and shows none. `layoutOf` keeps `over` and `mark` only while they name a field of the right kind, and `subtitles` only on a Media part. **It's derived, never trusted as kept:** `cardLayout.ts` → `layoutOf(fields, kept)` reads what's kept part by part (a broken bit reads as the field's default, a part for a field the type no longer has is dropped, a field added since joins at the end), and when nothing would show on the front the first part does. **A type nobody laid out looks as it always did** (`defaultLayout`): its front fields first, the first of them that holds words big and the rest quieter, then its back fields, which hold their room on the front, unseen. So every existing card looks the same until its layout is changed. **A field's side is now the layout's**: `typeFrom` sets each field's `side` from its part (`sideOf`: the front when the front shows it at all), and saving a layout writes the sides with it; what knows only two sides (a deck's card rows, a form's big field, the API's name for a card) keeps reading `side`. The Fields tab has no side control any more, and the old "a card needs a Front field" rule moved into the layout ("Put something on the front."). (`layout.ts` holds the shapes and imports nothing of `type.ts`, which takes `CardLayout` for its `CardType`; the functions that need a field's kind are `cardLayout.ts`, so the two never load each other.) **The stage tag is laid out apart from the fields** (2026-10-18, the owner: "obviously it's not a field, so it can work separately"): `CardType.tag` (`layout.ts` → `StageTag`: shown, size, corner; kept in `card_types.stage_tag`, read through `stageTagOf`, sent with the layout on the Layout tab's Save, its changes in `card_type_changes`). **The AI spark is laid out the same way** (2026-11-06, the owner: "make it toggleable in the card layout please, or movable like the stage label"): `CardType.spark` (`SparkMark`: shown, corner), kept in the same `stage_tag` JSON as its `spark` key, so no migration; `sparkMarkOf(kept, tag)` reads it, a type never laid out reading as it always stood (shown, in the top corner the tag leaves free: top left while a shown tag is in the top right, else top right); its rule of when it shows is unchanged (anything Panki AI wrote on the side shown, `byAi`). The two are one mechanism, **the card's corner marks** (`CORNER_MARK_IDS`, the tag first): `ReviewCard` → `CornerMarks` draws each corner as a row of the marks in it (`.review-corner`), in the registry's order, each a `CornerMarkSpot` (`.review-mark`); on the card being laid out every mark is drawn (faint while hidden, or while the spark has nothing to mark on the card), pressed to pick it (its choices in the panel: Shown, Corner, and the tag's Size), dragged anywhere to settle in the nearest corner, or moved a corner by the arrow keys, never set between corners. A new mark is an id there, its shape and kept key, and one entry in `CornerMarks`' list. The Layout tab's draft is the parts, the marks and Listen first together (`Laid`), so one Undo steps through all. **In a review each sound and clip answers to a key** (the same day): `ReviewCard` → `MEDIA_KEYS` (a, s, d, f…, by the layout's order across both sides, so a field keeps its key when the card turns), handed to `MediaView` as `shortcut`, which every look draws as the answers' keycap (`MediaKey`) and `usePlayback` listens for on the window (not while typing, nor with a modifier, nor on a key held down); none while a part is a spoiler still covered or held unseen for the back, none in the drawer (`shortcuts` is the review's and the Layout tab's, whose card is used as a review's).

- **Drawn by the one card** (`components/ReviewCard.tsx`). Each part is drawn where the layout puts it, on the side showing; rows are parts set beside the one above; runs of rows whose parts only ever show once the card is turned are one `.review-answer`, unseen on the front but holding their room, so turning moves nothing (a lone part like that in a mixed row is `.is-later`). A spoiler is the app's `Button variant="spoiler"` over the part, a plain block of its shape with no word on it (as Discord hides a message; the owner had the word "Spoiler" taken off it, 2026-10-15), its content `inert` and unseen under it; pressed, it's open until the card goes back to its front (`opened`), so a spoiler opened on the front stays open when the card is turned. **Playing by itself** (`autoplay`, which only the review passes): the parts in view whose `auto` is on and whose value is a sound or a clip are cued one after another in the card's order (`cued`, `played`; `MediaView` takes `cue` and tells `onCueEnd` when it ends, is stopped, or can't start), each once while the card shows, a spoiler's when it's opened; turning the card plays what newly came into view. The review keys the card by its id, so nothing carries over to the next card. **A reading over a word** (`over`, set on the reading by its "Reading above"): the reading is drawn as the named field's `<ruby>` (`readingFor`, `readingOver`) wherever the two stand in the card's order, with no place of its own then; its Size, Field name and Position are disabled (they're the word's to say), its Colour colours the `rt`. The ruby stands on a line of its own with the reading's room above it (`.review-ruby-line`), so a field name over the word stays clear, and its words are one box (`.review-ruby-text`) the same size covered or open, so opening a spoiler moves nothing (the owner, 2026-10-16, saw it jump, the cover being a block taller than the words); on the card being laid out a spoiler reading is covered as in a review. Both must be on the side shown and Text; one reading over one word, and a reading never has one of its own (a claim that doesn't hold leaves the part where it stands). Held for the back its `rt` is `.is-later`; a spoiler's words stay in the `rt` unseen with a `Button variant="spoiler"` laid exactly over them (`.review-ruby-covered`, the button `position: absolute; inset: 0`): a button in the line is a box of its own, taller than the words, and made the card grow until it was opened. While the card is laid out it's drawn the same, inside a `.review-ruby-pick` that picks the reading on a press (heard on the way down, after its word's part, so the reading is the one picked; a spoiler over it still opens), its name standing in while the card leaves it empty. `layoutOf` keeps an `over` only when it names another Text field of the type and the part is Text too. (Two tries came first the same day: a boolean `over` for "the one below", and `reading` on the word; both are read as nothing now.)
- **Two columns: the main one and the side one.** Any part can stand in the side column (`LayoutPart.column`; the Layout tab's Column row, Main / Side, `layoutKinds.ts` → `COLUMN_OPTIONS`; moving a part to the other column turns off its Position beside the one above, and a part is dragged only among its own column's parts on the side shown, `partsOnIn`, which column it's in being its own setting). On a review that fits a window 960 px wide or more, a card with something in its side column (its side column drawn: something there shows on the side shown or holds its room for the back; a field the card leaves empty draws nothing) stands as two: the side column at its left, `--clip-side` (52 %) of the card's width and as tall as the card, its parts centred and never scrolled, and the main column the rest, the one that scrolls. Anywhere else (a narrower window, a phone, the review filling the screen, the drawer's card, `CardPreview`) the two columns are `display: contents` and every block takes its place in the card's order by its first part (`ReviewCard` writes each block's `order`, below 0 so they stand before the card's hint), so the card reads exactly as it did with one column. In the full-screen rail the staged clip is fixed as ever and any other side-column part stands in the rail in the card's order. **The default is worked out, never written:** a layout kept before parts said a column (no part says one) has the first part showing a video in a picture (Player or Plain look) of a field that holds a clip on any card or example (`layoutOf`'s `holdsClip`, which the API answers from the data, `db.ts` → `selectHoldsClip`), standing in no row, in the side column, where the review drew the clip by itself before; a layout saved with columns keeps what it says. (A first build wrote the default into each type with a migration and was taken back; the migration list keeps a step that does nothing at 44 for the databases that had counted it, and what it wrote reads as a column the layout says.) The smaller word sizes beside a clip (`--beside-*`, on `.review-card`) are the main column's whenever the side column stands beside it. A player's bar wider than the side column wraps onto a second line (a review's side column is never narrower than 452 px; the Layout tab's card is the review's own, so it's the same).
- **What a file is, told apart.** `MediaView` draws a sound and a clip each in its own colour (`--sound`, `--clip` in `theme.css`, made of tokens every theme sets) and, when its part's name is on, the kind's icon (`MEDIA_ICON`: sound, film, picture) and the name on it.
- **Seven player styles for a sound or a clip** (2026-10-15: the owner, shown four new ones on https://claude.ai/artifact/2WEFUoj7rsgbJUp3KQB8yi, had them all built to choose from per field, and the browser's own kept as `plain` after trying it in place of Panki's player: "I liked the old player more"). **Full screen** is the player's own: a button at its bar's end (and a double-click on the picture) puts the player's frame, picture and bar together, into full screen (`requestFullscreen` on the frame; whether it's full is read off the frame's own root, a shadow root on someone else's page, so the extension's drawer behaves too), on `--theater` black. `MediaLook` is `player` (the bar, a clip's picture over it), `button` (the row's chip), `voice`, `round`, `tile`, `speaker`; the four new ones are `components/MediaLooks.tsx` and play a clip's sound alone through an `<audio>`. **The SRS in the web.** The words and looks are one registry, `srsKinds.ts` (the units, the steps a rule can be, how a stage, a place and a wait are phrased, a stage's colour as a tint: `tintOf`, `stageTint`, `placeTint`, `BURNED_TINT`); the rules in the contracts (`resolve`, `dueAt`, `waitNow`) are what the API runs, so the review's buttons say exactly where the API will send the card. `srsFlow.ts` edits a type without touching the one in state (every change returns a new type: set a step, wrap a step in an ask-again or flatten it, add or remove an answer or a stage with its rules cleaned up); `srsPreview.ts` walks a card through a type for the preview. **The editor (`SrsTypeView`) keeps a history** (past, present, future; a run of keystrokes in one field undoes as one) and **saves on its own** half a second after the last change, when the type parses; a change during a save queues the next; the header shows Saved / Saving… / Not saved yet (with the field's error under it) / Not saved. Hiding a stage with cards on it opens the question in the Stages panel and the next save carries the move. **The chart (`FlowCanvas`)** lays the rules out as a tree (one column per ask-again deep, one row per outcome, the stage or the default rules at the root), draws the arrows as SVG and the boxes as buttons on one sheet. **The sheet is laid out at the zoom (CSS `zoom`) and panned by whole pixels, not stretched with `scale()`**: a stretched layer drew blurred text and stray lines at the boxes' corners in Firefox, the owner's browser, at zooms like 96%; only under `G.sharpMin`, where browsers stop shrinking text, is the rest of the way a stretch. An icon inside a box is an svg too, so the arrows' sheet is styled as `.sheet > svg`, never every svg in it. An arrow runs from its answer's port through the answer's name to the box; pointer events on the frame pan it or drag a box or an answer's name (a name is kept as its distance from its port, so it follows its box; pointer capture, a 4px slack before a press becomes a drag, the click after a drag is swallowed), a native non-passive wheel listener zooms around the pointer (the ground is dragged to pan) and, over the rail, moves the stages sideways, the menu beside the picked box is placed from the box's geometry and clamped into the frame in a layout effect and closes on its ×, on a press on the ground that never became a pan, or on Escape. The boxes have no hover hints (removed at the owner's ask, 2026-10-10). **An ask-again can be capped** (`capped` on the step, optional so older rules read as not capped): `againWait` in the contracts gives the shorter of its own wait and the stage's (the card's current stage, never the next), and `resolve` and `waitNow` both go through it, so the API, the review's buttons, the preview and the chart all agree; starters and a newly added ask-again are capped. Box positions the owner dragged and the view live in the canvas's state per scope, and each scope is fitted to the frame the first time it shows. **The review (`ReviewView`)** builds its queue once from the cards' `due` and `dueAt` and the deck's `dueCount` / `newCount` (soonest due first, new ones as added or shuffled, in the order the Settings say), answers through the API, and a card that comes back within the hour is put back at the end of the queue. **Settings** saves each change a moment after it's made, a few quick ones as one.

**The frame is one CSS grid** in `app.css`, with named areas: on a PC the brand and the tabs share the top bar and the screen's own header sits in the 640px column; under the phone breakpoint the tabs become a bottom bar (sticky, above the safe area) and the screen's header becomes the top bar, the brand showing only on Home. One DOM, two placements. The SRS editor is the one screen that widens the column (`is-wide`, 1140px) and, at its own breakpoint, puts the preview beside it. While a field in the main column has focus the bottom bar is hidden (`:has`), because the keyboard is taking the room; what sticks to the bottom of the review sits above the bar by its height (`--tabbar`).

Fonts are self-hosted under `apps/web/public/fonts/` (Urbanist, two Latin subsets, OFL) and declared in `theme.css`; Japanese text uses the device's own fonts because CJK files are too large to ship. Nothing is fetched from a font CDN.

The mark (パ) lives as outlines in `src/brand/geometry.ts`, taken from Noto Sans JP at weight 900 so it is the same glyph on every device. `brand/Mark.tsx` draws it as four stacked SVG layers (the tile, the two strokes, the ring) so a moment can move each part; `pnpm icons` renders the same geometry into `public/icon.svg` and the PNGs the tab and the manifest use, with the colours read from the `--mark-*` tokens (the same in both schemes). **Animation is CSS keyframes, no library.** (The opening moment, `components/Intro.tsx`, an overlay on every page load, went at the owner's word, 2026-10-21; its `intro-pop` and `intro-rise` keyframes stay for the extension's "Added".) A library comes in only when a sequence can't be said in CSS.

**The board (chosen 2026-10-11).** The owner found the first Customize (a button, a one-column list) dated and the 640px column too narrow against WaniKani's density. Six ways of arranging were shown on a page (https://claude.ai/artifact/6XSxdM3bGPhz1mpSRWxjYo), then all built into the app behind a picker so he could feel each with his own data; he tuned the first, "A Hold", called it perfect and had the picker removed, and the other five (a ⋯ menu per tile, a tray, sizes dragged by a corner, sections under a strip of numbers, pins on Stats) came out of the code with it. What stands: the home and Stats draw one `Board` (`components/Board.tsx`); `is-wide` on both (1140px), three tiles across on a PC and one on the phone, 12px between, 14px inside, a title row with the widget's `control` text at the right. Given `home` (the doors, the layout, the arranging) it is the home's: the doors and the chosen tiles in one order, a door moved like any tile; a still hold on anything starts the arranging (`HomeView` holds that state so `App`'s header can carry Done), a × puts a tile away, the dashed tile at the end brings one back, a drop trades two places (`swap`), and it ends only on Done or Escape (a press on the ground used to end it, and a tile let go in a gap then closed everything). Nothing wobbles while it's on; the owner had that taken out. Without `home` the board is every widget in registry order, fixed. The layout is `layout.ts`: `{ order, hidden }` in `localStorage`, per device (the phone and the PC are set up differently on purpose), under `panki.board.hold`, the key it had while the six were tried, kept so what was arranged stays; read defensively (an unknown id dropped, a door not placed stands at the front, a widget the registry gained arrives put away, a broken value is the default: Streak, Past year, Coming up) and every write is a `layout` ledger event. Dragging on a grid is `hooks/useGridDrag.ts`, and since the owner's second note on 2026-10-11 ("I'm not actually dragging, I select and then it switches") the tile really is carried: on the lift a copy of the tile's own markup (`.tile-ghost`: cloned beside it so every style still reaches it, fixed, inert, without ids) follows the pointer, moved by its `transform` straight from `pointermove` with no render in between. The hook's `lifted` and `over` are state, so `Board` draws the order as letting go right now would leave it (`dropped(from, to)`, the same function the drop itself commits, so what was shown is what is kept), the lifted tile lying faint in its landing place; the layout is written once, on the drop. Whatever moved to make room slides there (the places are read before the order changes and after, and each thing is animated from the one to the other with `Element.animate`), and on release the copy flies down onto its tile and is removed. A press lifts after a still `hold`, or at once on a pull where `eager` is on (a board being arranged); a finger's pull only lifts a tile whose `touch-action` is `none`, since anywhere else that move is the page's. Pointer capture is taken on the root, not the tile, because the tile's node moves as the order changes; `touchmove` is prevented and `contextmenu` suppressed while lifted, and the click after a lift is swallowed. A list is still `useDragOrder`'s (the type editor's fields, the SRS editor's stages). The home's header is action-only (`Head.title` optional), for Done.

**Themes (step 14).** `themes.ts` is the registry (id, name, scheme, the two swatch colours) and the only place a theme is listed; `theme.css` has the light set on `:root` and one `:root[data-theme="…"]` block per theme giving every token a value, with no `prefers-color-scheme` media query any more: "Match the device" is resolved in JS (`matchMedia`) into `light` or `dark` on the root, by an inline script in `index.html` before first paint (so nothing flashes) and again by `applyTheme` once the app runs, which also keeps a `matchMedia` listener alive only while `system` is chosen and sets the `theme-color` meta from the theme's surface. The choice is `localStorage` `panki.theme`, per device, and each change is a `theme` row in the ledger.

**Signing in, in the web (step 10).** `App` boots by calling `GET /api/me`; until it answers the frame is bare (`is-bare`: the brand alone, no tabs). No answer at all (the server restarting after a deploy, nginx's `502`, the network gone) isn't a sign-out: the column says "Can't reach Panki right now. Trying again…" and asks again every `UNREACHED_RETRY_MS` until it answers (until 2026-10-20 any failure showed the sign-in screen, and a deploy looked like a lost sign-in). A `401` from it, or from any later call, goes through the one hook `api.ts` exposes (`onSignedOut`): `App` drops everything loaded, clears the unsaved-work guard, stops the ledger and shows `LoginView` in the column; the address is kept, so a sign-in lands where the owner was going. `LoginView` is a `Form` of two `TextField`s (the password one by the field's `type` prop, with `autoComplete` set so the browser's password manager fills them) validated with the contracts' `Login`; the API's message shows under the form. `me` (the user and the signed-in devices) is held by `App` and handed to `SettingsView` for the panel at the very bottom: a Sign out per device (the current one through `POST /api/logout`, another through `DELETE /api/sessions/:id`) and Sign out everywhere, which asks first in `Ask`'s small panel hanging from it and then `DELETE /api/sessions`. Signing the current device out is the same path as a `401`.

**The ledger, in the web (step 9).** `ledger.ts` queues `NewEvent`s (`record(kind, extra)`) and sends them as one `EventBatch` three seconds after the first or at twenty waiting; on `visibilitychange` to hidden and on `pagehide` it sends what's left by `navigator.sendBeacon` (in `api.ts`, the one place that talks to the network), because a fetch may not outlive the tab. It records nothing while signed out and drops its queue on a lost sign-in. `App` records `app-open` once sign-in is confirmed, `screen` with the path on every screen shown (an arriving address records once, when it's reached), and `layout.ts` records `layout` on every change to the home's board, `cardSort.ts` records `sort` on every change to how a deck's cards are ordered; the ledger's own listener records `app-show` / `app-hide`, installed before the beacon so the hide rides in it. **The review reports its sitting:** `ReviewView` mints a `ReviewSessionId` and `POST`s it on mount (a failed start never blocks the review; the answers then go without an id), times each appearance of a card with `performance.now()` (on screen → flipped is `thinkMs`, flipped → answered is `decideMs`, both sent with the answer, clamped to the contracts' max), and ends the sitting as the screen goes (or the page, `pagehide`), with `keepalive` so leaving doesn't lose it: `left` with the card still up and how long it had been up, `done` at the end screen (not as the line runs out, since 2026-10-26: Undo there takes an answer back into the same sitting); Extra study ends the day's as `done` and starts a fresh `extra` sitting. StrictMode's double mount is covered by a deferred "left" that the remount cancels.

**Stats and the tiles (steps 12 and 13).** One registry, `widgets.tsx`: every widget is an entry (`id`, `name`, `wide`, `home`, `render(stats, ctx)`), drawn inside the one `Widget` tile shell. `StatsView` fetches `GET /api/stats` (or `?deck=`) for the chip picked and draws every entry in registry order, keeping the last numbers on screen at reduced opacity while the next load; all the numbers are the API's, the web only divides for a percentage. The heat map is an SVG of 53 × 7 cells, Sunday at the top of each column, four steps of one hue (`--heat-1…4` in `theme.css`), the day under the pointer said by the app's `Tip` and by the line under the chart (never an SVG `<title>`, which is the browser's own bubble). The stage bars take their colour from `tintStyle` in `srsKinds.ts`, the same ramp as everywhere. `HomeView` draws the doors and the chosen entries on the `Board`, from the stats `App` fetches for all decks whenever the home is shown; the Review door is `is-hot` only while `dueCount + newCount` over every deck is above zero, and the deck's Review button is `primary` on the same rule for its deck. How the home is laid out and arranged is "The board", above.
