# Transcribing a video

> **Scope of this file.** Transcribing a video: the engine, the modes, the chunks, the transcript and its editing.

A video is transcribed by an engine (ElevenLabs Scribe v2) called by Panki's server, never the extension; automatic, following the viewer; one transcript per video for everyone, in the shared store. It's the one exception to "nothing generated" (`conventions/data.md`), and the transcript is named as Panki's wherever it shows. Panki's settings (Settings → Admin) cover what costs (engine, speed, lead, chunks, modes, limits); the viewer's own (trigger, mode pick, over the video, subtitle style, source) are this browser's (`kept.md`). The owner's word for a chunk is "chunk": "follower" and "piece" are code words, never said to them.

## Modes and the whole video

- **Two ways, combined by modes** (`packages/contracts/src/transcription.ts` → `TRANSCRIBE_MODES`, `modeOf`, `wayOf`). **Full**: the server asks the engine to fetch the video by its address (`source_url`) and transcribe all of it in one call. **Live**: the chunks, below. **Hybrid**: chunks from the first second, the whole video downloading behind them; when it's in, its words replace everything and the silent player stops. An admin sets the default and switches modes off (the default can't be off); a viewer picks among the rest. A mode named in a sentence is always `ModeChip`.
- **The download limit** (`downloadLimit`, `overLimit`: Allowed / Not allowed / Warn first). Over it, Warn first opens the menu on the question with the cost; Not allowed refuses, and the server refuses too (`POST …/whole`, `403`). A video waiting on that answer is `limitPending`: the status button wears a "!" until the menu is opened.
- **Cost is the engine's listed price** (`ENGINES`: `creditsPerMinute`, `centsPerHour`), never measured. Only time is estimated from Panki's own data (`shared.ts` → `estimate`: over the last 30 days, the middle of each download's seconds per minute and how soon a run's first words came); with nothing measured it says "No data yet". The engine's credit count read either side of a download is kept as its record, never shown as a cost.
- **The download** (`apps/api/src/transcribe/jobs.ts`): in the API's background, one at a time per engine, its deadline scaled to the video (`DEADLINE`); tried once more after a trouble that may pass (`EngineError.passing`: unreached, 5xx, 429), **never after a deadline**, since the engine may have done and charged the work; a download the API stopped under is taken up once at the next start (`resume`). Done, every earlier piece of the video stands aside with its words (and the hand fixes on them: the latest overwrites), and its words are kept in pieces of about 30 s (`piecesOfVideo`). A chunk that arrives after, over what the download covers, isn't sent to the engine (`coveredByJob`).
- **Falling back**: a failed download (YouTube refuses the engine's fetch of some videos) falls back to the chunks in every mode (`fallBack`): Hybrid's carry on; Full's start from where the viewer is. The download is asked again on the next visit. Each visit asks for the whole video once whenever some of it isn't transcribed (`maybeWhole`, every look, so a mode changed mid-video asks too); an earlier transcript's words don't stop it.

## The silent player

`apps/extension/src/transcribe/silent.ts` → `createSilentPlayer`. The video is opened a second time in an `<iframe>` of the page (the embed page; the watch page when the embed shows `.ytp-error`), off screen at a player's size, `allow="autoplay"`. Same origin as the page, so its `#movie_player video` is reached directly: nothing is injected, nothing of the site's is called, only the element is used.

- **Unheard, never muted**: its sound goes into a sound graph of the frame's own (`createMediaElementSource`) and through a `GainNode` at 0. A muted element gives the graph zeros.
- **At speed**: `playbackRate` is the setting and `preservesPitch` off, so the sound is the video's own, faster. A `ScriptProcessorNode` takes it (a worklet is a module fetched from an address, and an extension's script on someone else's page has none the page allows) and hands it on as if at `sampleRate / speed`, which puts the voice back at its pace with the top of the range gone: 3× keeps exactly the 16 kHz the engine hears, 4× loses a little above 6 kHz, which is why the speeds stop at 4 (`TRANSCRIBE_SPEEDS`).
- **Time is the video's own, placed by the sound clock**: `currentTime` and the context's time are paired at `playing` and every `timeupdate`; each block says when it was rendered (`playbackTime`, less a block of latency, plus `outputLatency` + `baseLatency`), which places it (`videoTimeAt`). **A piece's start and end are read off the block they fall in** (`pieces.ts` → `timeOfSample`), never counted on from the piece before: a count carried from piece to piece drifts from the player's clock and the words come ever earlier through a run.
- **The clock is calibrated against the one sure thing**: after a seek the graph gives silence until the player is there, then sound from exactly where it landed (`seeked`'s `currentTime`, not the moment asked: the site seeks again to the whole second its address named). The first non-zero sample is the video at that moment; the clock's error there (the browser's latencies and buffering, which no reading states) is kept over the last `BIASES` seeks and the middle one taken off every reading. An error past `ONSET_TRUST` is the video's own silence and isn't learned from. Every block is watched for the onset from the seek on: one already thrown away puts the words late.
- **YouTube moves it**: as its page loads the site may seek to where the account left off; the silent player puts it back once that seek has landed (**never from inside the seek handler**, which left the site's player playing out loud), up to `GUARD_TRIES`. A player the site swaps into the frame is muted and paused at once and the silent player gives up (`REPLACED`), so nothing outside the graph is ever heard. One stuck loading `STUCK_SECONDS` is reopened where it stands, up to `REOPENS`. The cutter closes an open chunk at any untold leap (`CUT.leap`) and drops anything before 0:00.
- `pause`, `waiting`, `seeking`, `stalled`, `emptied` and `ad-showing` drop the pair; nothing is taken then.

## The chunks

`transcribe/pieces.ts` → `createCutter`, `CUT` (whose comment is the full account of the numbers and their trade-offs).

- **One unbroken recording**, cut in the middle of the first half-second with no voice once long enough, never shorter than `minVoice` of voice, force-cut at the quietest frame of its last seconds when too long, the next beginning `overlap` before that cut. **A pause isn't a seam**: `suspend()` keeps the open piece and `anchor()` goes on filling it. A voice is a frame louder than `ratio` × the noise floor (the quietest tenth of recent frames).
- **The ramp** (Panki's settings, `nextChunk`): a run's first chunk, and the first after a jump, is `firstChunk` long; each next `chunkGrowth` times the last, up to `chunkLength`; closed at the first pause past that, or force-cut `pauseWait` later. Skipping over what's transcribed isn't a jump. `PIECE_MAX_MS` is the longest a piece can be.
- **Quiet is covered too**: a piece's `startMs` is where listening began untold of, its `soundMs` where its sound begins, so the quiet before it is covered with it; a stretch of nothing alone goes as a piece with no WAV (`tellQuiet`), a `transcript_quiet` row and no call. Otherwise every silence is a hole in `covered`, which the follower takes for something still to transcribe.

## The follower

`transcribe/transcriber.ts` → `createTranscriber`, `FOLLOW`, looked at every `FOLLOW_MS` with the viewer's player.

- **Starts by itself** when the trigger is Auto, the source is Panki's and there's a key, once the video plays and `FOLLOW.settle` has passed since the video changed: the silent frame boots on the page's own thread, and both loading at once froze the page. A press doesn't wait. It starts from where the viewer is (`FOLLOW.back` earlier, so the first word is whole), at the first uncovered moment (`nextUncovered`).
- **The lead**: the silent player is held `lead` ahead, but **only once the chunk being recorded there has been cut and sent**: the lead is words ahead of the viewer, never sound held back unsent (`pending`). Let go once the viewer is `slack` closer.
- **Following a jump is judged by where the viewer is at every look**, not by a change between two looks: a jump made while the player was loading is otherwise lost. It skips anything transcribed ahead (`coveredUntil`). A fix or a forget sets `resync`, so a run goes back for what's uncovered behind it.
- **The language**: a run not started by a press asks the engine's verdict piece by piece until settled (`Run.settled`): `japanese` settles it; `other` stops the run; `unsure` (a jingle, a silence) leaves the next piece to ask. Transcribe forces it.
- A run follows the viewer or covers a stretch in the background (`Run.scope`). Stop blocks auto-starts until another video plays.
- **Re-transcribe a part** (`retranscribe`, `steerRedo`): the part widened to whole pieces, recorded end to end in its own run whatever the mode, and on past its end to the next pause (`Cutter.closeAtPause`) so it meets what stands after it in a pause; each chunk kept apart under the part's id, and stood in place of what was there only once every chunk is back (`askRedoEnd`). Anything that interrupts it gives it up, the transcript as it was.

## The source

`subtitles.ts` → `SUBTITLE_SOURCES` (`panki`, `youtube`, `none`), this browser's pick. `engineWanted` is whether the pick is Panki's: only then does anything transcribe, and a run ends the moment the pick leaves it. **Nothing falls back**: with Panki's picked, where the transcript hasn't reached the line shows nothing and YouTube's caption window stays faded. The strip names the kind of subtitles, never the engine.

## What the line reads

- `transcribe/lines.ts` → `linesOf` turns words into lines in the captions' own `Line` shape, so everything that reads captions reads a transcript; a transcript line's id is negative, which tells them apart. A line ends at 。！？, at a long pause, at a shorter pause once the line is long enough (a hesitation mid-sentence doesn't cut it), or at a length; **a split or join kept on a word** (`LineMark`, below) is obeyed before anything else.
- `transcribe/lineSource.ts` → `createLineSource` is the strip's one source, by the pick. `LinesView` is worked out on every frame for the video's own time (`youtube.tsx` → `followLines`; a slower tick shows a line late), the `FOLLOW_MS` tick still calling it for a tab whose frames aren't drawn. `saying` is held `HOLD` after its last word, as a subtitle lingers.
- **Over the video** (`OVER_VIDEO`, a place inside `#movie_player` under its controls): the line being said in this browser's subtitle style (`subtitleStyle.ts`, put on the host as CSS variables). The band is laid out as a flex row with its height the host's `bottom`, so where it sits never depends on what the player inherits into the host. YouTube's caption window is faded (`CAPTION_AREA`, `showingOver`) **the whole time the setting is on and Panki's is the pick**, between lines and before the transcript catches up, or the site's captions come back in every gap.

## The strip's Transcription menu

`ui/TranscribeMenu.tsx`, opened from the status button (`.strip-status`). The status words cover a video's first moments as **Loading**, until how it stands can be said, so the button doesn't flicker through states. `useDismiss` hears the press on the shadow root as well as the window: a closed root hides its insides from the window, so a window listener alone closes the menu on every press inside. Details are the transcriber's `detail()`, **pulled every half second while open, never pushed** through `tell`, which would redraw the strip as often. The Log (`note`, `LogEntry`) is every decision with its reason, kept per page, copied from the menu and printed to the console as `[Panki] …`; the server logs a download's steps, never the key.

## Editing by hand

One mechanism, two ways in (`ui/TranscriptEditor.tsx`): the strip as the editor (`StripEditor`, from the menu or a word double-clicked under the player) and the subtitle over the video (`OverLineEditor`, a word of it double-clicked; one click still picks a word for a card).

- **`LineText`** (`ui/LineText.tsx`) is a real `<textarea>`, its text clear, over a drawing of the same text in which Panki AI's words are lit with a spark that takes no room; typing, undo, paste and Japanese input are the browser's own. The textarea and the drawing must share font, wrapping and padding exactly.
- A line is kept as the caret leaves it (`askLine`). The server matches the typed text to the line's words letter by letter (`contracts` → `realignLine`: longest common run, each word taking the letters from where its first landed to where the next's did), moves each word's times with the line's ends, and every word changed is a fix; the engine's word stays beside it (`TranscriptWord.heard`).
- **Lines never overlap**: ends that would reach over a living word of the line before or after are refused whole (`shared.fixLine` → `overlap`, `409`); the nudges stop at the neighbours' ends.
- **A split or a join** is kept on the word it happens at (`LineMark`: `POST …/line-marks`, `transcript_line_marks`), the latest standing; a `break` always starts a line there, a `join` never. A line's `key` is its first word's id, which keeps the focus on it as lines are cut again around it.
- Panki AI's edits on a line and their Undo: `ai.md` → "The transcript's place: Proofread".

## Through the worker, and the server

- A piece goes as base64 (a message carries no bytes) to `PUT /api/transcripts/:videoId/pieces/:pieceId` with the WAV as the body, **under an id the extension mints**, so a piece sent again is the same piece and never a second engine call. A `409` is read as no key.
- The engines are one registry (`transcribe/engines.ts`); the key (shared, else `PANKI_ELEVENLABS_KEY`) goes only in ElevenLabs' `xi-api-key` header, never logged. `language_code` is always the account's language; the language the engine says it heard is kept with the piece and decides nothing.
- **The stitch** (`shared.ts` → `insertPiece`): two overlapping pieces (a forced cut, a jump) both heard the word at the edge; by geometry alone, the piece that heard a moment farther from its own edge keeps its word and the other's stands aside (`removed_at`), so exactly one survives at each moment and no overlap is named in the protocol. `covered` is the kept pieces' and the quiet's stretches merged (`MERGE_GAP_MS`).

**Unproven:** the frame's player starting, and its sound graph running, without a press (`blocked` shows it; Transcribe retries from a press); `createMediaElementSource` getting an MSE-fed player's sound (if not, `captureStream()` on the frame's element is the next way); ads in the frame; 3× with `preservesPitch` off sounding plainly faster, not pitch-kept; Firefox throughout (`mozPreservesPitch`, `createMediaElementSource` in a frame).
