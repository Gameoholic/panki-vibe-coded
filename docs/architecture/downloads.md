# Downloads: a video kept in this browser to watch without signal

> **Scope of this file.** Downloading a video: the doorway script, the hidden player kept, what's kept and how it's ordered, one page at a time, the store, the menu, the options tab, the downloaded mark, and playing from the copy. The plan and what's next: `docs/DOWNLOADS.md`.

A video is downloaded by copying what YouTube's own player hands the browser to play, while the video plays through fast in a hidden frame. Nothing is fetched by Panki and nothing of YouTube's is called or changed: the copy is the pieces themselves, untouched, in the quality YouTube sent.

| Part | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `content/doorway.ts` → `doorway.js` | The doorway script: in the site's own page (`world: "MAIN"`), every frame, `document_start`; copying in the frame with the download mark (`keep`), playing from the copy in the page's own frame (`feed`), nothing in any other |
| `download/mark.ts`, `download/bytes.ts` | The words in the frame's address and on each message; bytes as text and back, a start piece told; neither imports anything, so the doorway script carries nothing else |
| `download/playback.ts` | On a downloaded video's page: what to put into the player from the copy, and when (`createPlayback`, `PLAYBACK`) |
| `download/download.ts` | Every tunable number (`DOWNLOAD`), the qualities, the shapes, and the stretch arithmetic (`coveredOf`, `firstGap`, `gainedOf`) |
| `download/runner.ts` | On a YouTube page: asks for a download to run, runs it in the hidden player, sends each piece on (`createDownloads`) |
| `download/store.ts` | The extension's own IndexedDB `panki-downloads`, the worker's and the options page's alone |
| `download/words.ts` | A download in words, the same in the menu and on the options page |

| `ui/DownloadedMark.tsx` | The downloaded mark on a picture and on the player bar |

## The doorway script

- **The browser's one standard way for a page to play streamed video** is Media Source Extensions: the player makes a `MediaSource`, a `SourceBuffer` per stream (`addSourceBuffer` with a MIME type), and appends the bytes it fetched (`appendBuffer`). The script wraps those three (and `changeType`) on the prototypes, in the frame only: each piece goes to the browser untouched, and a copy, taken at once (the player may reuse its bytes), is posted to the parent page once `update` says the browser took it in, with the stream's number (its `MediaSource`, counted), its length, its MIME type, its `timestampOffset` and append window, and its buffered stretches before and after. Bytes travel as base64: a string crosses from the site's world to the content script in both browsers without Firefox's wrappers, and the worker needs text anyway.
- **It answers nothing**: what the player asks the browser (`MediaSource.isTypeSupported`, `MediaCapabilities.decodingInfo`) is the browser's to answer. Answering "can't play" for video taller than the quality was tried and left the hidden player never loading in Chrome ("The video wouldn't load in the background"), most likely because the player asks those questions at large sizes to learn which kinds it can play at all, and told no it plays none.
- **The quality** is the hidden frame's size alone (below): the player, picking for itself, never loads a picture much taller than it's drawn. A **ceiling only**: it picks lower when the connection can't keep up at speed, and that's what's kept. Forcing the pick would mean calling the site's own player.
- **The focus stays the viewer's**: the site's page in the hidden frame focuses its own player as it loads, which took every key (the arrows) from the player being watched. The moment the focus lands in the frame, `silent.ts` (`keepFocusOut`) gives it back to what had it, the page's player when that's gone; transcribing's hidden frame alike.
- **Copies only on the mark**: `pankiKeep` (the run's random id) in the frame's address, which only `silent.ts` writes, and only for a download. In the page's own frame it's the feeding side (below); in any other frame it reads the address and stops. It posts only to the site's own origin.

## Playing from the copy (B, on trial)

- **On a downloaded video's watch page** (downloaded whole, Panki on, `youtube.tsx` → `playback`), `download/playback.ts` looks every `PLAYBACK.lookMs`: it asks the doorway script for the player's streams (the latest source with both streams started, its length the video's, so not an ad's), and where a stream holds less than `lowSeconds` ahead of where the video plays and the player has put nothing into it for `quietMs` (no signal; with signal the player keeps itself ahead, and its pieces are its own to put), it puts the kept pieces that come next into it, a step of up to `stepSeconds` at a time, until it holds `aheadSeconds`. A step is a run of one stream's pieces (`stepFrom`), each with its own offset and window, all read with the same start piece: the latest of its stream before them in the order they came in, as kept. The bytes come from the worker a piece at a time (`downloadFacts`, `downloadBytes`), and cross to the page as JSON strings.
- **The doorway script's side** (`feed`): from `document_start` it wraps the doorway in the page's own frame, notes each stream the player makes and the player's own latest start piece in it, and otherwise passes everything on untouched. Asked to put a step in, it waits for the player's own call under way, lets go anything half put in (`abort`), changes the stream's kind of file to the copy's if it differs (`changeType`), puts in the copy's start piece and the pieces, then the player's kind and its own start piece back, and its settings (`timestampOffset`, the append window) as it left them. Out of the player's sight all along: its calls on that stream (and its source's `endOfStream`, `duration`, `removeSourceBuffer`) wait their turn and are made right after, in order; `updating` reads as the player's own call alone; its settings read as it left them; and the stream's events for Panki's pieces never reach its listeners (`addEventListener` and the `on…` handlers, wrapped). Whatever went wrong is the answer's `trouble`.
- **What it did is said**: in the page's console as `[Panki] playback: …` (how many pieces are kept, each step put in and how far the player then holds, a step it refused and why, where the copy has nothing, the player starting its streams again, and the player's own error, by the video element's `error`), and its latest words under Downloaded in the panel's menu.
- **Not A**: nothing of Panki's plays the video. If YouTube's player throws away what's put in, or stops with an error when its own requests fail, that's what's said, and A (Panki's own player over the site's) is the owner's call.

**Unproven, all of it until tried:** that the player plays on through what it didn't put in itself rather than stopping when its requests fail; that it doesn't throw away or overwrite what's put in; that a piece of its own half put in when the signal dropped is let go cleanly (`abort`, then its next piece read from the start); that a copy of another quality or kind plays on from the player's own (`changeType`, a new start piece); that a JSON string posted by a content script reaches the page's own world in Firefox with the page's origin.

## The hidden player, kept

`transcribe/silent.ts` → `SilentUse` `{ kind: "keep", mark, height }`: the same frame, stray guard, stuck reopen and ad detection as transcribing, on **the watch page alone** (`PAGES`: in a frame off the screen the embed page refuses ordinary videos without showing its card, error 152-4 once it's in view, and every refusal cost a download its first seconds), with the mark in the frame's address, the frame the quality's size in screen pixels (16:9, `height / devicePixelRatio` tall, since the site never loads a picture much taller than it's drawn), and its sound into the gain of zero with no tap (the watch page can't be opened muted, and muting the site's player is kept by the site for the viewer's own videos; a graph the browser leaves suspended is as unheard, and isn't taken as blocked). A download is whole only where the picture and the sound both cover (`coveredOf`). Until the player has a video in it, it hasn't started (`hasVideo`): told to play with none, the element reads as playing. Played at the Download speed (`DOWNLOAD_SPEEDS`, 1× to 16×, 4× to begin with, kept as `downloadSpeed`), read by the worker as each run is taken up (`claimDownload`, `DownloadJob.speed`); a run going on keeps the speed it began at. Nothing of a download hangs on its sound being heard (no tap, the graph's suspension not taken as blocked), so Firefox muting a video past 4× changes nothing: the site's player fetches and appends the sound to keep its buffer either way. Past what the connection gives, the site's player loads lower qualities, or waits, and a wait past `STUCK_SECONDS` is the stuck reopen. **Only once the page has been pressed** (`pressedHere`, `navigator.userActivation`): before that Chrome won't let the frame start a video with sound, and the site's player in it never asks for the video ("no mode, no source"); a download is taken up from a page only after a press there (Download itself is one), as a transcribing run waits for the viewer's video to play. A player told to play that still has no video `NUDGE_SECONDS` later has its own play button pressed, once, and the browser asked to play it (`silent.ts`, both uses). Nothing waits on the viewer's own video playing: Download pressed with it paused starts the same. How starting went, step by step, is the report's `starting` (the page loaded, a video in the player or none, play asked and what the browser answered, the play button pressed or not found): the Waiting line while it hasn't started, and the reason when it's given up, up to `TROUBLE_MAX`.

## What's kept, and in what order

- **One page at a time** (`claimDownload`): every YouTube page asks the worker every `claimEveryMs` while it runs nothing; the worker hands out the oldest unfinished download not waiting to be retried, only while no page holds one, and marks it held by that tab (`heldBy`, `heldAt`). Each piece kept, and a word every `holdEveryMs`, renews the hold; one not renewed for `holdMs` is free for any page, and a page whose hold went elsewhere is told to stop at its next word. A page opened again in the same tab takes its own back at once. So a download goes on as the viewer moves between videos (the frame lives in the page across the site's own navigations), and the next YouTube page takes one up after a tab closes.
- **A run** needs the player's sound fetched while it's muted (unproven; if not, the run stays at 0% with bytes coming in) and starts at the first moment not covered (`firstGap`), skips a stretch kept by an earlier run when it reaches one (`skipMs`), and at the video's end, once every piece on its way is kept, goes back for what's missing, twice at most per part. It's whole when both streams cover the video but its last `endSlackMs`.
- **Only the video's streams**: a piece of a stream whose length is known and more than `durationSlackMs` off the video's is left out (an ad plays in a `MediaSource` of its own); with the length not known (the site may never set it, and the browser then says none or an endless one), only while the hidden player shows an ad. Never held back waiting for a length: that left every piece waiting, 0 bytes kept.
- **Where it got to** (`whereOf`): the doorway script also says it woke (`awake`) and each stream the player makes (`DoorwayNote`); a run that has kept nothing says which step it's at (opening, the copy script not started, an ad, the player not started, no stream made, nothing handed over, every piece another stream's), under its row in the menu and in the page's console as `[Panki] download: …`, with the hidden player's strays and stuck loads. Stopped for copying nothing, that's the reason its row waits with; given up loading, the reason carries how the site's player stood (`stuckWords`), which of the site's pages was open, and whether the copy script woke.
- **Each piece** (`KeptPiece`) is kept under `[videoId, seq]`, `seq` the video's own count in the order pieces arrived, with its stream (`video` / `audio`, from its MIME type), its MIME type, whether it's a **start piece** (by its first bytes: a WebM's EBML header or an MP4's `ftyp`; what a player must be given before a stream's frames), the stretch it added to its stream's buffer (`gainedOf`, null for a start piece), the offset and window, its size and when. Its bytes are a `Blob` in a store of their own under the same key, so listing never reads them. A start piece the player hands again at a seek is skipped when it's the same as the stream's last.
- **What playing reads** (`download/playback.ts` → `stepFrom`): a stream's frames by the stretch each added, each after the latest start piece of its stream before it in `seq` order, so a quality switched mid-video (a new start piece) is followed.
- **Stopping** (`holdDownload` `stopped`): the hidden player failing, blocked, nothing copied in `nothingSeconds` of video, or a part that won't come in; the download waits `retryAfterMs(tries)` (30 s, then 1, 2, 5 and 10 min) before any page tries again, its reason shown as Waiting. A piece kept clears it. Nothing stops for good by itself: offline looks like any other failure.

## Downloading On / Off

Each browser's own (`Modules.downloading`, kept as `downloadOn`, On to begin with), the Downloads tab's first row. Off, nothing new is taken or handed to a page (`claimDownload`), a download running is let go at its next piece (`releaseDownload`), kept as it stands, and goes on at once when it's switched back on, with no wait; the panel menu's Download this video is greyed with why, and the Download automation isn't asked, its Log line saying so (`[Panki] automation: … downloading is off in this browser`).

## The store

`panki-downloads` (`videos`, `pieces`, `bytes`), in the extension's own origin, room unlimited (`unlimitedStorage`). The worker writes (`downloadPiece` arrives as base64, decoded there, `DOWNLOAD.pieceMaxBytes` the most one may be), removals from the options page included, so it can tell every page; the options page reads directly, as it does the dictionaries. How a download stands (`stateOf`: done, going while held, waiting), how much is in and its size are worked out from the pieces on every read, never kept. Remove deletes the video's three stores' rows by key range: these are a copy of YouTube's, not the owner's data. Nothing goes to Panki.

## On screen

- **The menu** (H Menu): Download, between Panel and Panki. Download this video (greyed in an ad or before the length is known); Downloading / Waiting with the share and a thin bar (`--p`), the reason under Waiting; Downloaded with its size. Pressed while there, `Ask` hangs from it: Remove. Downloads… opens the options page's Downloads tab, with how many and their room. The menu reads the list again every `WATCH_MS` while open (another page's progress); the page running a download overlays its own progress live.
- **The options tab**, all this browser's as everything on the extension's options page is (no "This device" tag, which is the web's settings' alone): Downloads (a bar of each one's share of the room, the total, a row each with its still, name, channel and state, Remove asking first, sent through the worker so every page hears of it), Quality and speed (Download quality, a `Segmented` of `DOWNLOAD_QUALITIES`, kept as `downloadQuality`, copied onto each download as it's asked for; Download speed, a `Segmented` of `DOWNLOAD_SPEEDS`, its line under it saying a faster speed past the connection costs quality) and Mark (Downloaded mark, a `Toggle` Shown / Hidden, kept as `downloadMark`, `Modules.downloadMark`). The still is a frame of the page's own video as Download is pressed, a small JPEG kept with the download: no thumbnail is fetched.
- **The downloaded mark** (`ui/DownloadedMark.tsx`, the `downloaded` icon): only on a video downloaded whole (`DownloadsNow.done`, from `stateOf`'s done), on the player's dark glass at a picture's bottom left, clear of a progress bar. Its consumers: every thumbnail of the site's (`feed.tsx` → `sayOf`, drawn as text in the thumbnail's shadow root with the icon's own strokes, `iconPaths`, beside the saved tick), Continue watching's lead and cards, Saved for later and Recently watched (`feed.tsx` → `Downloaded`), the recap's what's next (`Recap` → `downloaded`), and the player bar (`DownloadedControl`, `BAR_CONTROLS.downloaded`, after Save for later; shown only on a downloaded video, out of an ad). The setting hides it everywhere, and with Panki off it's off (`allOff`); on the bar it's also a row of the bar's own, shown or hidden and moved as every control is. Left without it: the drawer's YouTube field (the card's own facts about the video on screen, which wears the mark on its bar) and Panki's own site (its tiles can't see this browser's downloads). **Live**: the worker tells every page (`DownloadsChanged`, `background.ts` → `tellEveryPage`) whenever a download is asked for, finishes or is removed, and each page's runner reads the list again; the bar is looked at every second, the rest follow the runner's `subscribe`.

**The watch page plays as the account**: the frame shares the browser's sign-in with YouTube, so YouTube can count the hidden play as the account's (its own watch history, and its own resume point, the red bar on its thumbnails, moved to where the hidden player stops), as the embed page can too; neither is called or prevented here, and which YouTube really does for a frame off the screen is unproven. Panki's own watching (`watching.md`) reads the viewer's player alone and isn't touched.

**Unproven:** that YouTube's new error card still carries `.ytp-error`; that the watch page in a frame starts with sound unpressed (a refusal shows as "play refused (NotAllowedError)"); that the site's player appends on the page's thread (MSE in a worker would leave nothing to copy: "YouTube's player gave nothing to copy"); that the frame's size keeps it to the quality; whether it sets its streams' lengths at all; what the pieces look like in each browser (expected: separate video and audio streams, VP9 or AV1 in WebM or MP4 for the picture, Opus in WebM or AAC in MP4 for the sound, a start piece first and again at seeks and quality switches); `world: "MAIN"` content scripts and the base64 posts in Firefox; a frame playing at 4× in a tab in the background; the hidden frame's quality off screen.
