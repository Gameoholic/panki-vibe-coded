## What this is (and why)

Panki is the owner's replacement for Anki, for Japanese vocabulary (kanji stays on WaniKani). The owner has studied Japanese for two years with WaniKani and Anki, slacked off for a month, and is building their own flashcard app because they don't like Anki's UI. It runs in the browser on their Windows PC and their Android phone, from a small VPS they already pay for.

**The one goal: the seconds between seeing a word and having it in a deck.** Sentence mining never worked for the owner because adding a card took a minute; every time they found a word in a video or a conversation, adding it was so tedious they gave up and ground their base deck instead. A feature that doesn't shorten capture or make reviews worth coming back to doesn't belong.

### The pain points it fights

- **Adding a card is tedious** — the biggest one. The whole reason sentence mining never happened.
- Anki's UI.
- A messy Google Doc of slang and words picked up from friends.

## Current product shape

### Steps 2 and 4 — decks, and getting around

**Home is a hub** with four doors: **Review** (the big one, with how many cards sit in how many decks), **Decks**, **Add a card**, and **Card types** (wide, under them, naming the types). **Home and Settings are the two fixed tabs:** a bar across the top on the PC, along the bottom on the phone, always one tap away. Everything but Settings is behind the hub; every other screen has a back arrow and a title at the top. Every screen has its own address, so the browser's back and forward, a mouse's side buttons and a phone's back all go where you were before, and a reload stays on the screen (the owner's ask, 2026-10-10). Chosen 2026-10-09 as "5 · Hub with tabs" from five navigations shown live (https://claude.ai/artifact/WbU1c46RvY5JMvdXVEyJwr): the owner liked the tabs of one option and the hub of another, and wanted nothing under the deck list.

**Decks.** The list of your decks, each with its card type's name and its card count; press one to open it. "New deck" is in the header; it opens a form above the list with the name and the card type (the first type is picked already), creates the deck and opens it straight away. Nothing sits under the list. Cards you made before decks existed sit in a deck named "Default".

**Inside a deck.** Back to Decks and the deck's name; then two tabs, Cards and Settings. **Cards:** under the tabs, Add card and Review side by side, then the deck's cards, newest first. "Add card" opens the form above them, one field per field of the deck's card type, the optional ones marked (Enter adds it and the form stays open for the next one; Cancel closes it); an empty required field is pointed out under it in Panki's own words. The owner asked (2026-10-09) that the form not sit on the page until asked for. **Settings:** see step 5.

**Review.** The Review door asks which deck; one deck at a time, by the owner's call. Then it flips through that deck's cards in a shuffled order, the word first, tap or press Space to reveal the reading and meaning, then Next; "Start again" reshuffles. Back returns to wherever the review started: the deck's cards, or the picker. That was the review until step 8, below, gave it a schedule and answers.

**Add a card** from the home: the deck is picked in the form (a row of chips, the first deck to start with, the last pick remembered while the app is open), then Word, Reading, Meaning; what you added this visit stays under the form. Before any deck exists, Review and Add both offer the one way forward: make a deck.

**Settings** is its own tab from the start, with nothing to set yet.

There is no login and no card editing yet, a deleted deck can't be brought back from inside the app yet, and refreshing the page returns to Home.

### Step 5 — a deck's settings

**The Settings tab inside a deck** holds the deck's **name** (Save turns on once it differs, Enter saves, the title changes straight away) and, at the bottom, **Delete**. Delete asks once, inside the panel, with what it costs: "Delete 'Mined' and its 31 cards?", Keep or Delete deck. Nothing is destroyed: the deck and its cards disappear from Panki and stay in the database with the time they were removed. **Each row in the Decks list has a settings button** that opens that deck straight on its Settings tab. Chosen 2026-10-09 as "1 · Tabs" from four styles shown live (https://claude.ai/artifact/SB79am6JvmbT5banZbe5yp): the owner said "1 is best"; the row button, the hide-not-destroy delete and the kept rename history are the standing rules applied.

### Step 6 — card types

**A card type says what a card is made of:** a named list of fields, each with a name, a kind (what it holds; step 7), a side (Front: what you see before you flip; Back: what the flip shows; since 2026-10-15 set on the type's Layout tab, below) and whether it's required. The first Front field is the big one on the card. **Every deck is made with one** and keeps it; the deck's cards, its add form, its list and its review all take their shape from it. **Card types** is the fourth door on the home: the list of types with their fields and how many decks use each; open one to rename it, or to rename, reorder, add or hide its fields and change their kinds and sides; a change reaches every deck on that type. A new type starts with one Front field. A type can be deleted only when no deck uses it; hiding a field keeps whatever was typed into it.

**The starter type "Word"** (Word front required, Reading back, Meaning back required) is what every card was before this step; every deck from before has it, and it's a type like any other, to rename or reshape. **No "Japanese" setting on a field:** the app looks at the text and uses the Japanese font when it's Japanese.

Built 2026-10-09 straight in the app at the owner's ask ("just do it in the main build and I'll judge it"), from the mock at https://claude.ai/artifact/4yhn8JD8cA9bryEwwimSU8. The name "Card type", the field's three parts, the starter type and the door are the defaults of that mock, to be changed if the owner says so.

### Step 7 — what a field holds

**Fields are reordered by dragging:** hold the grip beside a field's name and move it, with the mouse or a finger; the arrow keys on the grip do it too. The owner asked for this on 2026-10-09 in place of up/down buttons.

**Each field has a kind.** **Text** is one line. **Paragraph** is a box of several lines, for a sentence or notes: it grows as you type, Enter makes a new line, Ctrl+Enter adds the card, and the lines are kept on the card and on the review card. The kind sits beside the side in the type editor, in one line with **Required, now a switch** with its label: on or off at a glance, the same height as the kind and the side. Every field from before is Text. The owner asked for both on 2026-10-09: fields "can have types" (audio and video put off as complicated), and the Required chip was out of line and unclear. **In the editor, Enter moves to the next field and adds one at the end,** so a type is typed out in one go; Save is the button, or Ctrl+Enter. (Enter used to save and leave the editor, which the owner read as "going back" when trying Paragraph.)

### Step 8 — the SRS

**An SRS type says how a deck's cards come back.** It is a ladder of **stages**, each with a wait (minutes, hours, days, weeks or months); two to six **answers**, the buttons under the review card (four until Bury and Suspend joined Wanikani+'s, 2026-10-21); **rules** for what each answer does to the card — up or down the ladder, straight to one stage, Burned, *ask again* after a wait and let the next answer decide, as deep as you like, **Bury** or **Suspend** (below), or **Button hidden**: the button isn't there at that point of the chart (on the chart no box follows it: the answer's name has an X before it and is struck through corner to corner, top left to bottom right, at the end of a dashed stub, since a box read as what pressing it did, the owner, 2026-10-18; Wanikani+ hides Easy in both its asked-again branches, right away and the next day's second chance; asked for 2026-10-17 so Easy isn't offered inside an ask-again, "there's no reason to bury a card if I already failed it", and Wanikani+ does that since 2026-10-18; the other answers keep their number keys; it was "Not offered" for a day, which didn't say the button goes); and the end of the ladder: **Burned** (the card never comes back), or the card keeps coming back at the last wait or with the wait growing. The rules are the same for every stage unless a stage is given rules of its own ("Sync to default" takes them back). **SRS types** is the fifth door on the home. **New comes before the first stage, in every type** (the owner, 2026-10-21: "a card can never go back… it will be rock bottom at Apprentice 1"; it was called Lesson for its first hours, then New: "new cards also makes it explicit", and it is one word everywhere, the card's tag, the chart, the stats, "New cards a day", the review's count): it's where a card not learned yet stands, and it has rules of its own on the chart (its chip on the rail after Default rules, "new cards"), no name or wait to set. A new card waits for the day's new cards and counts against them. Leaving New is learning it; once out, a card goes no lower than the first stage, and only a **New again** step (a chip on any box's card, which no type that comes with the app uses) takes it back, as a new card in every way, its history in the silo still saying what it was. A card's place says "New", with nothing under it in a deck's list. In Wanikani+ and Wanikani, Ok learns it and Forgot asks again until it's got right, then learns it (the owner's yes: WaniKani's lesson); a type made before has rules for New worked out from its answers until it's saved. **The first stage waits like any other**, set on its box on the chart like any stage's. Any stage can also be switched to **No wait** on its box (asked for 2026-10-10, "useful for the lesson"): a card landing there is due at once, for the next review (since 2026-10-17: reaching a stage ends the card for the sitting under way, only "Ask again right away" keeps it in); under the wait the box says what it counts from ("since added" on the first stage, "since promotion" on the rest). A card kept on it or sent back to it comes up in the next review, not this one. (The owner on 2026-10-10: "if I forgot in the lesson, then got it, it will come up in the next review"; it had no wait before, which read as "back right away" and confused.) **Three SRS types come with the app and can't be deleted** (the owner, 2026-10-10: the templates stop being templates and become real types): **Wanikani+** (his own rules, below), **Wanikani** (as WaniKani does it: a miss keeps the item in the review until it's right, then it drops by ceil(misses / 2) stages, doubled from Guru up, never below Apprentice 1; counted to four misses) and **Anki** (its default deck laid on a ladder: New and one learning step, Learning (it was two, Learning 1 and Learning 2, until New took the first one's place, 2026-10-21), then a day and about 2.5 times more each stage, and 1.5 times more each time past the last one (the owner found 2.5 there "too high"); how much a ladder grows past its top can be set to one decimal; Anki's per-card ease can't be held by fixed stages, so it is the nearest thing, not the same thing). They are for looking at and for starting your own from, not for editing (the owner, 2026-10-10: "make the default ones view-only"): the screen shows the preview, the chart (any stage can be picked, and since 2026-10-21 every box opens its card as an editable type's does, every setting shown and none to be changed: the owner, "I'd like to be able to click Suspend and see the other options… still read only"), the answers with their two switches, as set and locked, and the stages from New to the end, and a button to start a new type from it; the API refuses a change to one. Wanikani and Anki say only "Ported from WaniKani." and "Ported from Anki." about themselves. In the preview, pointing at a day of the "Reviews a day" chart says that day's count. **A new type starts as a copy of any type in the list, or Empty**, which is literally empty: no stages and no answers (the owner's call), not savable until it has a stage and two answers, and its preview says so. **The "Reviews a day" forecast asks how often you review** (once, twice or three times a day, or as soon as anything is due; once a day to start with) as well as the pace and how far ahead, because assuming every card is answered the moment it comes due was not how the owner reviews. The list is one list, the owner's own types first; the ones that come with Panki follow under a thin line, drawn quieter with a lock (option B of four shown on 2026-10-10, https://claude.ai/artifact/S1RBY9xt4xWTkYW548sirj; the two headed sections before it he disliked). A stage whose own rules come back to the default ones, however it got there (a box removed and put back counts), follows the default again and is no longer offered "Sync to default", and a type that comes with it shows no delete section at all. **A type has a description** in the owner's words, shown under its name beside the line worked out from its stages (Wanikani+ says what it adds to Wanikani: the one-day forgiveness from Guru 1 up, Easy, Reset, Snooze and Suspend). **The editor saves when the owner says so:** Save is in the header, beside "Unsaved changes" (the owner moved it up from the bottom and had the Discard button removed: "I can always just click to go back"). Leaving with unsaved changes (the back arrow, a tab, the browser's back) asks first in a pop-up over the dimmed page, the one place the app has one, at the owner's ask: Stay, Discard changes, or Save and leave; a reload or a closed tab gets the browser's own "leave?" question. There are no drafts: unsaved work is on the screen and nowhere else, and a lost connection or a crash loses it, which the owner chose over a draft system he tried and disliked (2026-10-10). **Wanikani+, as the owner set it out on 2026-10-10:** its Lesson was a stage with no wait ("lessons should have no wait since being added") until it became every type's own, before the first stage, on 2026-10-21 (New, by the end of that day). Forgot keeps the card in the review, asked again and again until it's got right ("Keep asking", a step that asks the same thing again without moving; on the chart it has no box: that answer's arrow goes out through the answer's name and turns back, with a head, into the ask-again box it left, because a box of its own left it unclear what happened next; a tap on the answer's name opens its card). Once it's got right, an early stage (Apprentice 1 to 4) drops one, never below Apprentice 1, as WaniKani does; from Guru 1 on the card gets one more chance first, asked again tomorrow and never later than the stage, and only a second Forgot drops it, after that one too has been asked until it's right (the owner's yes, 2026-10-10; those four stages have rules of their own). Two more answers sit apart from Forgot and Ok (the owner's names): **Reset** sends the card back to the first stage, Apprentice 1, to climb again, and asks before it's applied (it was **Relearn** until 2026-10-21, and for a few hours sent the card back to New; the owner: "make it Reset… puts it into the first stage after New"), **Easy** lifts it two, at once (it asked first too until the owner had that off, 2026-10-21) (it was **Bury** until 2026-10-17, renamed because in Anki that word means "hide until tomorrow"; the owner liked its sound and may want a button of that kind one day). **Bury and Suspend** came on 2026-10-21, Anki's two, as the owner put them: **Bury** hides the card for a while, its schedule untouched ("for a card you can't judge right now, a bad clip, a tired evening"): 24 hours to begin with, set on its box on the chart in minutes, hours, days, weeks or months (the owner, the same day, after asking how it differed from "Ask again tomorrow": "time based… default is 24 hours"; for its first hours it lasted until the day turned). Unlike an ask-again, the card doesn't move into a box of its own: it comes back where it was, following its stage's rules, and nothing about it is counted; its box on the chart says "Bury for 24 hours" over "then comes back as Guru 1" (the stage the chart is shown for; the owner's words); **Suspend** takes it out of rotation until it's brought back from the deck's list ("for leeches and cards that need fixing"). In Wanikani+ they sit apart with Easy and Reset, in the order Easy, Snooze, Suspend, Reset, keys 3 to 6 (Easy moved to third and Reset to last at the owner's word, 2026-10-22), and like every answer set apart there they ask for confirmation, a held key being the yes. Neither moves the card or counts as an answer in the stats; a card that fell due while it was aside is due the moment it's back, and a new card put aside doesn't use up the day's new cards: the next one takes its place. Bury isn't offered inside an ask-again, for the owner's reason ("there's no reason to bury a card if I already failed it"); Suspend is, since a card failed again and again is the one to take out and fix. Under each the answer says what happens ("back in 24 hours", "until brought back"); on the chart either is a step any answer of any type can take, with its own box. In the deck's list a card put aside says "snoozed" or "suspended" where its due time was, with **Unsnooze** or **Unsuspend** under it. **Bury is called Snooze everywhere on screen since 2026-10-21** (the owner: "I think it makes more sense that way"): the answer, the chip and the box on the chart ("Snooze for 24 hours"), the state and the button in a deck's list. Any answer of any type can be set apart or made to ask first, with two switches under its name in the editor; on the review screen the ones set apart are a quieter row under the main ones, and the preview's sums leave them out. **The only thing asked again in the same review is "Ask again right away"**, a step of its own beside "Ask again later" on a box's card (and a switch on an ask-again's card). Three starters come with the app — **WaniKani-like** (a Lesson stage, then eight from 4 hours to 4 months, then Burned; Forgot asks again tomorrow before it drops a stage; every deck from before starts on it), **Classic** (a start, 10 minutes, then a day to 8 months, and the wait keeps doubling; four answers) and **Gentle** (a start, then a day to a year, and it keeps coming back) — and a new type starts from one of them or from Blank. A type can be deleted only when no deck uses it.

**The editor is a chart.** The stage (or the default rules) on the left, an arrow per answer to the box that says where the card goes, and an ask-again box that branches again. Tap a box for a small card beside it: the five moves along the ladder in one row (↓ 2, ↓ 1, Stay, ↑ 1, ↑ 2), the rare ones as chips (Burned, ask again later, ask again right away; "back to the start" was one until the owner had it removed on 2026-10-10, since a stage's dot does the same), and the stages as numbered dots in their own colours, with the name of the one pointed at or picked written above them. Another tap on the box, a tap on the ground or Escape closes it. (The owner disliked the tall scrolling list it was before and picked this, option B, from four shown on 2026-10-10: https://claude.ai/artifact/FuiR58QQd3S1AVdPhGmKba.) Drag any box around, the first one too, and an answer's name along with its arrow (the owner's ask on 2026-10-10); press + on an arrow to put an ask-again before it, drag the ground to pan, scroll or the buttons to zoom (the owner's call on 2026-10-10: scrolling zooms, it doesn't move), Fit and Tidy up. A rail across the top picks which rules are shown: the default ones, or a stage, whose own box then holds its name and its wait right on the chart; a stage with its own rules is marked with a dot; when the stages don't all fit, the rail moves sideways (a finger, the wheel over it, or picking a stage) and fades at the end that has more. Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Y redoes. **An ask-again is never later than the stage itself** when its switch is on (the owner's "min(tomorrow, current)", 2026-10-10): "tomorrow" on a two-week stage, four hours on a four-hour one, and ten minutes on a ten-minute first stage (the owner's correction, 2026-10-10: the cap is the current stage, never the next). New ask-agains and the starters have it on; the box shows the wait a card on the shown stage really gets. **The default rules are shown for one stage, and the owner chooses which**: tap the Default rules box (it carries a small down arrow) and pick a stage from the list that drops beside it (the owner asked for a dropdown, 2026-10-10); the boxes then say where a card at that stage would go and when (asked for 2026-10-10; it's a way of looking, not saved with the type). The boxes carry no hover descriptions and an ask-again box has no second line (the owner found both unnecessary, 2026-10-10). **Every change is saved on its own** a moment after it's made — the header says Saved, Saving…, or Not saved yet with the field's error under it. The **live preview** sits beside the chart on a wide screen (above it on a phone): one card's journey answered with the best answer each time, and what the type costs each day over three months at a pace picked in the preview itself. Under the chart, **Answers** are named in a row (two to four), each name in its answer's colour (the owner didn't like a colour stripe on the field's left edge, 2026-10-10), and **Stages** are rows reordered by dragging, with **Burned as the last row** (named, removable; without it, the line under says what the last stage leads to). Removing a stage that cards sit on asks, in place, where they go. Chosen 2026-10-09 as design A, the flowchart, from three designs on one model (https://claude.ai/artifact/YHNuguX7xnUtN5ZCQozYNC, then the full-screen page https://claude.ai/artifact/QAiWraXJtBuQDwJ8yn6CPt through eight rounds of the owner's changes); built 2026-10-10.

**Review runs on the deck's SRS type.** A session shows what's due (soonest first) and what's new for today, as many as the Settings allow; the Review door, the picker and the deck's Review button all say how many. The card says where it stands (New, its stage, Burned) and how many times it's been seen; reveal, then the answers under it say exactly where each sends the card and when it comes back ("Apprentice 3 · 1 d", "again tomorrow", "Burned ✓"), keys 1 to 6 on a PC. Only a card asked again right away comes back in the same review; anything with a wait, even ten minutes, is for the next one. The end tallies the answers and shows what's coming up tomorrow, in two days, three, this week. Each card row in a deck shows its stage, coloured by its place on the ladder, and when it comes back.

**A review opens on today's cards** (the owner, 2026-10-26, picking B of three ways to peek and pick: https://claude.ai/artifact/1sSZsVseDpRJpDQE1F2u62). Before the first card, today's reviews and new cards are tiles, each with its word and where it stands, every one picked. A press leaves a card out for the next review (it stays due, or new, untouched); a stage's chip, All and None do many at once; Every new card shows the deck's other new cards, to learn the ones you want past today's count. Start (or the Show answer key, or Enter) begins the review on what's picked, in the deck's order; picking nothing out, it's the review it always was. A deck's settings have it as Today's cards before a review, Shown to begin with; Skipped goes straight in, as before.

**Undo takes the last answer back** (the owner, 2026-10-26). The button sits beside Chart (in the top row when the review fills the screen, and on the end screen too, so a wrong press on the last card can be fixed). The card comes back on its front, as if it had just come up, and the counts and the tally are as they were; pressed again, it takes back the answer before that, all the way to the start of the review, Snooze and Suspend included. Leaving the review, or starting Extra study, ends what can be undone. If the card was changed elsewhere since (answered on the phone, say), it says so and changes nothing. Its key is in Settings → Keybinds, none to begin with (the owner: "I can set it if I want"), and like every key there it can be one pressed with Ctrl or Alt, Ctrl+Z among them. It's its own thing: the editors' Ctrl+Z (the SRS chart, Layout, the popup's layout) is untouched. Nothing is erased: the answer is kept, marked as undone, and counts for nothing but the seconds spent on it.

**The top of a review counts what's left as three numbers**, "2 new 9 reviews 0 again", each number in its own colour with its word after it and the kind of the card on screen underlined (the way Anki shows it, at the owner's ask, 2026-10-10; it replaced "5 cards to go · 7 answered"; the words came 2026-10-21, when the owner found "2 + 0 + 9" unclear). Left to right they stand in the order their cards come, by the deck's settings: new before the reviews, or after them when new cards come last; the ones asked again last when they go to the end, between the two otherwise. As in Anki, the middle number counts answers still to give, not cards: a card being asked again counts every answer it takes to leave the review if each is the best one. The card no longer says how many times it has been seen (removed at his ask). **When a review ends and the deck still has cards never started, the end screen offers "Extra study"** (his name for it): more new cards today, as many as the owner sets there (the deck's daily count to begin with); the day's count is a pace, not a wall (his ask, 2026-10-10). **With one deck, the home's Review goes straight into it** (no picker to press through; back goes home). **A clip's full screen fills the screen with the whole review** (the owner, 2026-10-25: "I usually always do them in full screen mode… a smaller condensed version of the card"; "it won't exit full screen for the next card"): the clip takes the screen and the rest of the card stands in a **rail** at the right (A of https://claude.ai/artifact/LJ8v6mgrDgiDhyQnh7Zpee; glass over the clip's corner, B, was tried beside it for a day: "rail is better"), all of it fitting on the screen with no scroll bar ("it all needs to fit in one page"): the card's words a size smaller, the answers two to a row. × hides the card, leaving the clip alone, until you next go full screen ("it will simply reappear"; no button to bring it back); the answers' keys work all the while. It stays full screen as one card gives way to the next, and the device remembers it, so a review hours later starts in full screen too (at once, or at the first key pressed when the browser won't fill the screen unasked). Esc or Leave full screen ends it, and that's remembered too. ### Step 15 — a sound, a clip or a picture on a card

Built 2026-10-11, the first of the five steps that lead to the extension (the plan, with the owner's calls decided: https://claude.ai/artifact/D3jDa6x2TLya8Z68geVBZc). The extension needs somewhere to put the line's clip; this is that place, and it works by hand too.

**A field can hold a file.** Beside Text and Paragraph a field's kind can be **Audio**, **Video** or **Image**. With five kinds the choice no longer fits in a row, so in the type editor the kind is a small menu ("Text ▾") beside the side and the Required switch. In the add form such a field is a dashed button, "Choose a file", that opens the device's own file chooser; on a PC a file can be dropped on it instead. Once picked the file shows right there as it will on the card, with its name, Change and a × to take it off. A file Panki doesn't take, or one too heavy, is said under the field at once, in Panki's words, and the card isn't added without it when the field is required. Panki takes mp3, m4a, ogg and webm for sound, mp4 and webm for a clip, jpg, png and webp for a picture; a sound up to 10 MB, a clip up to 40, a picture up to 5.

**On the card.** A picture is drawn in a frame. A sound is a bar: a round play button, a line that fills as it runs, the time. A clip is its picture with the same bar under it; pressing the picture plays and pauses too. On the review card a file stands where its field does, front or back; pressing its player doesn't flip the card, and Space still flips as always (since 2026-10-19 back to the front too, once the card is turned; no key shown for that). In a deck's list a picture is a small thumbnail and a sound or a clip is a small chip with the field's name that plays the sound, so a long list stays light. The big word of a card is its first Front field that holds words, wherever a file stands.

**Where the files live.** On the VPS beside the account's database, given only to a signed-in device of that account. Nothing is generated and nothing is fetched from anywhere: a file is what the owner (or, from step 17, the extension) put there.

Not yet: a key that replays a sound in a review, and sounds that play on their own when a card comes up; each is a small thing to ask for.

## Design language

**Chosen 2026-10-09: "A · Paper"**, from three looks shown live (https://claude.ai/artifact/XxoXCFUSbQwWyJzV1rhDFw — B "Ink" was typographic with no boxes, C "Console" dark-first game-feel). What A is, as built: a light neutral ground, white bordered cards, **Urbanist** (self-hosted) for Latin text and the device's own Japanese font, a quiet indigo accent, 12px radius, counts as small accent pills, section labels in small uppercase, the review word at 40px on a card with a soft shadow. The dark set follows the system for now.

The principles underneath, carried from the Board:

- **Clean, modern-SaaS:** a light neutral background, white bordered cards. Not themed, not skeuomorphic.
